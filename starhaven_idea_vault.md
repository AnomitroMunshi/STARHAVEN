### §0c. The south-west frontier (added 2026-11-23)

New in the Atlas: **Saddlestone Keep** (new fort, outer ring finished autumn 1059, second ring still scaffolding, no commander yet — Iva Renn acting warden; "Khulan's Rule": garrison doubles when the field armies march, never below full strength at harvest), **the Kessrin Marches** (empty grass kept for a battle; the Border Road, the Watching Hill, the Night Cairn, the Long Grass, the Harvest Beacons and Beacon Night), **Bridlewick** (horse village; Kessrin-looking horses at the spring fair; the Two Saddles inn).

The hook: the Kessrin tale *The Rider Who Would Not Dismount* — Khulan waited 22 years for her enemy's army to march away at harvest, then burned the harvest. Behind the Marches lies the Southern Agricultural Corridor, most of Starhaven's bread. The lone rider only ever comes in spring, never at harvest.
- **Commander (recommended):** Edda Rensh, named at the end of *The Rout She Walked Out Of* — the commander who walks a broken line out intact is the one you want on a border where the enemy fights from the saddle. Until then the chair stays empty.
- **Payoff:** the 1060 Silver Isle War sends the armies over the sea at harvest time → the Kessrin finally ride (Khulan's raid), and the Harvest Beacons and the Long Grass get used. Raise it at the Winter Council first as a weak point ("the south-west has one unfinished fort").

### Suggested city (1060 onward): Goldwake(open to chaneg name) 

**Site:** The first red-circled area, between Saddlestone Keep and Veilhold Keep, near the Saffron Terraces and the Ashfield road. This spot can connect the farm villages to the frontier keeps and the southern road. Leave the second marked area open for a different future settlement.

**Origin:** In 1060, a Crown survey crew begins laying an all-weather route between the frontier posts. A cut into a low ridge opens a sealed gate and reveals the upper streets of a city buried centuries ago. Its old aqueduct failed after an earthquake shifted the underground watercourse; the city was abandoned, not Void-corrupted. Surveyors then find a modest but rich gold-bearing seam beneath its old mint district.

**Why rebuild it:** Goldwake gives Starhaven a second domestic gold source after the Winter Council identifies its reliance on the Sable Gold Concession. The find is valuable enough to support a city, but not so large that it replaces Sorvane's concession or turns the kingdom into a gold rush. The old stone roads, cisterns and sheltered foundations make rebuilding practical; nearby orchards, vineyards and saffron farms can feed a growing market town.

**How it forms:** 1060 begins as a guarded survey camp and a careful archaeological dig, not a city built overnight. A public charter reserves the old quarter and aqueduct for restoration, permits staged mining under the Worker Rights Compact, and directs a share of the gold revenue to the road, clinic, granary and schools. Miners, masons, merchants and farm families settle around the recovered gate. Over the next several years, the camp grows into a chartered city with a market square above the old mint and new homes built around—not on top of—the exposed ruins.

**Story hook:** Mining deeper threatens the aqueduct that keeps the surrounding farms alive. The city must decide whether to chase the richest vein or preserve its water and the old city's remaining districts. Working name **Goldwake**; alternatives: **Gildhollow** or **Aurelfall**.

### Wider regional strength and expertise [agreed direction, planning only]

- Starhaven should not be the world's only source of exceptional people or institutions. Each major region should have its own notable strengths and influential figures, including experts beyond rulers, Lords and Solo Legends: scholars, archaeologists, mages, engineers, navigators, healers, historians, scouts and artisans.
- Scope the balance pass across every named realm, island and people, alongside Starhaven's seven mapped macro-regions. Treat cities, forts, districts and individual map sites as part of their wider region, not as separate power centers.
- Existing external figures include Eirathra Veyl Frostborne of Frostspire, Sorvane Nullis of the Sable Isles, Aldric Vorn of Ironmere, Crest Vane and Hesper Quillon of the Thalassian League, Rashid al-Kavar, Aria al-Kavar and Zafir of Karath, Brokk Graniteborn of Stonemark, Drogar Stonefang and Kragh Skullsplitter of the Highland Coalition, Rudra Vanar of the Vanari, Cassian Silverhorn and Elowen Sunspire of the Verdant Crown, Zahid al-Morad and Nessa Korrath of Zharak, Kadrin Emberforge of Irondeep, Tharos Avarakai of the Reef Clans, Ilyana Sanguinar of Karvossa, and Elder Dravan and Kira of Varathos. ### Water resilience: Winter Council → Goldwake(open to chaneg name) [agreed direction, planning only]

- Treat water security as a separate weakness from the single Ashen river route, which is a transport vulnerability. The Council commissions a survey to map groundwater and identify how much Starhaven relies on a few known river sources.
- An Arcanum water mage with a water-finding ability joins the survey. The ability points to promising sources; engineers still test safety, quality and replenishment before anyone relies on a well.
- The Crown recruits the proposed male field archaeologist Ronan Haldvik from the Frostspire Dominion for a permanent research post. He has mapped ice-sealed archive passages and buried underworks; his remit covers hidden underground structures, sealed spaces, buried routes, relics and physical evidence connected to Void history. He works closely with Velkan, bringing field evidence and material context to Velkan's arcane research; he is not himself a mage. A joint scholarly appointment and Starhaven's preservation charter give him reason to make the move. His work is not limited to this survey or one arc.
- Goldwake can be one early field project in 1060, not the reason for creating or recruiting him. The water-finder helps locate the buried watercourse; Ronan leads the careful excavation after the road crew opens the sealed gate. The restored aqueduct and farm supply must be protected from the gold mine. His wider work continues across underground and Void-related discoveries in multiple regions.

### The Last Fare — all-women mercenary company

Five provisional Dummy Legend cards are grouped under **The Last Fare** in the main wiki. This independent company grew out of a caravan escort crew. They take contracts to bring people home, list each passenger by name, and collect half the fee when the last person arrives.

- **Mireya Valcobre, the Fare Captain** — bronze-armored swordswoman. On stone she can drive the force of a parried blow into the ground and use it to hold a crossing until the last wagon is through. Her fox's little bell tells people in hiding that she's come back for them.
- **Zurra Keldruk, the Doorbreaker** — white-haired hobgoblin hunter who learned to break open homes buried by snow. She earned her mammoth-tusk war hammer, **Cairnwake**, in the **Hanging Vault**, an A-rank dungeon, when she stayed behind to free a pinned delver. **Stilled Blow** stores up to three strikes against one object and releases them together when she taps it again.
- **Nerilda Tavrel, the Lamplighter** — former mine surveyor whose crescent staff, **Noonwick**, banks sunlight. **Sun Under Stone** lights a dark cavern or hall as brightly as midday for thirty minutes. **Sunwash** uses that stored sunlight to cleanse Void corruption from people and surfaces. She can also brace a failing arch or spend a full charge splitting a stone obstruction along an existing fracture.
- **Iscara Moltren, the Waymarker** — forest archer in a horned skull helm. With **Last Span**, she can shoot an arrow from **Wayback Yew** into a solid anchor and raise a bridge up to sixty feet long for one minute. The bridge can carry six adults at once. She crosses last and pulls the arrow to drop the span behind her.
- **Ruzena Kestovar, the Last Seat** — Wolf-Kin rear guard with a tall shield and flail. **Three Minutes of Fang** turns her into a great wolf with overwhelming strength for three minutes. The passenger she once held a seat for over three days is now the company's quartermaster.

**Possible first appearance:** On the road to Saddlestone Keep before harvest, a village pays the company to escort one wagon out. Mireya writes down nineteen names, counts twelve seats, and asks the reeve which seven people he meant to leave behind.

## 1. Arc: The Silver Isle War (working title) — set after Year 1059

**Premise [Ano]:** A land/island with a huge silver mine. Starhaven's spymaster finds it. Another (new) kingdom wants it too, got there first, and is killing the natives or enslaving them. Starhaven fights for the silver, makes a deal with the natives, frees them from slavery, and the island comes under Starhaven's protection.

**Beats [Ano]:**
1. Refugees from the island reach Starhaven.
2. Through them, the spymaster learns about the mine and what is happening there.
3. Starhaven attacks the occupier.
4. Starhaven makes a deal with the natives: silver in return for freedom from slavery.
5. The island becomes part of Starhaven's protection.
6. During the war, **Dante** fights as a mercenary on the natives' side. It is his birthplace, and the natives were already fighting. Starhaven fights alongside him, Rayden likes him, and asks him to join Starhaven.

**The four-armed people [Ano]:** A new race lives on the island. Their original island sank. Their king is unknown and has not been found. He is "the Architect" and is found later.

**Link to the Severim [Ano — "same one"]:** The Architect is connected to the Void's four-armed Void Architects (the Severim in the Nullborn registry). Their origin is still to be decided. See open questions.

**Why it fits the wiki (checked) [Suggested]:**
- Silver: Starhaven buys almost all of its silver from House Silverhorn's Greymantle mine. A second source removes a single-supplier risk. Gold depends largely on the Sable Gold Concession, held under Sorvane Nullis's mining rights.
- Slavery and freedom match the Worker Rights Compact (Year 1043).
- Veyra Tal's *Treaty Before the War* ability and the Fourth Field Army suit the deal-making half. Dawnfleet and Tidebreaker Corps suit the landing.

**Cast:**
- **Tolliver Quarrow** — the spymaster who finds the mine (see section 3).
- **Dante** — merc, native son, later joins Starhaven (section 2).
- **Rayden Cale** — meets Dante in the field.
- **The Architect** — the unknown four-armed king, found later.
- **[Suggested]** Veyra Tal / Fourth Field Army for the treaty; Dawnfleet for the sea landing.

**Open questions:**
- Island name (no "Ash"; check the wiki).
- New rival kingdom's name, ruler and slave system.
- How the Architect connects to the Severim (the island people corrupted by the Void? A separate origin that Starhaven mistakes for them?). The Severim "appeared at Ashfield with no documented ancestor" in the registry, so one of those two wordings would need to change.
- Does the sunken island connect to the Vrak? (Possible, not decided.)
- Does Tolliver come from Treasury (Astrya Lenn) or from Nyx's Web?
- Is House Silverhorn told? It is an ally and the current silver supplier.

## 6. More ideas from wiki gaps [Suggested — added 2026-11-23]

Found by counting how thinly the wiki covers each topic. Top picks: **#2 and #7**.


1. **Traitor in the Web (top pick).** The wiki has no "treason" or "rebellion." Every threat is external. Arc: a loyal-looking minister quietly sells information to King's End or Vel'Nathar, and Nyx must hunt a traitor inside his own network.
2. **Malgrath himself.** The Void's king has only about 6 mentions in the main wiki. Possible: Drevak's second return, or someone who has spoken with Malgrath. Pays off the Pale General's sealed document.

4. **The next generation.** Year 1059's "Unlicensed Party" (Tarin Varric, Leo Holt, Liana Holt, Roen, Tavi Dusk, plus a dire wolf pup) growing up in the 1060s could be a sequel arc.
5. **Moral dilemma for Rayden.** The rival kingdom from the Silver Isle arc offers peace if Starhaven gives up the island. Tests whether "protection" is just conquest.
6. **The Vrak and the flying fortress (top pick).** About 28 mentions of the Vrak. The fortress "remembered" the fleet, and no one knows whether more exist or whether the Nullborn know. Arc: Starhaven tries to contact them. Possible tie to the Silver Isle's sunken island (see section 1).
7. **Silver cold war with House Silverhorn.** Once Starhaven has its own silver, its ally loses leverage. A political arc with no battles, balancing the war-heavy chronicle.
