### §0i. The Nullborn must win something — Ironpass night siege (agreed 2026-11-27)

So far the Nullborn have lost every fight on the page (First Ashen Passage War 1048, The Long Winter 1057–58, When the Thread Breaks 1059) — high cost each time, but always a Starhaven win. The registry makes them frightening; the stories must too. Canon already sets this up: Nyx's note that Ashfield was only a *vanguard*, and that the Nullborn "introduce units we have no doctrine for, and watch how long it takes us to make one."

In **Zharak Crossing + the night siege of Ironpass**:
- **Nyx's Coordinator decapitation fails for the first time** — the **Signal Keepers** (registry: "didn't exist at Ashfield, which is why that decapitation worked") cut the four-second window to staggered two-second seizures.
- **Ashfangs** hunt the Ironpack by its whistle frequencies (registry: the Ironpack switched channels; use that tension).
- **Grath'Uel returns** (registry: "He will return") — larger, and remembering Rayden.
- Never-before-used units: **Void Burrowers** under the walls, **Hollow Crawlers** through the drains, **Rift Blades** hunting commanders, a **Walking Bastion** at night.
- **Ironpass's outer works fall in the dark.** Ironpass itself holds — only just.
- **A named Starhaven commander dies.** A real loss, not a near miss.
- Hold back the **Malgrath-Touched Titan** and **Drevak** ("larger than he was") — glimpsed, not used.
- End on the Nullborn having *learned* something Starhaven has not yet answered — feeds the Winter Council ("bare Void borders", "night").

### §0k. Powerhouse gaps vs the Nullborn (agreed 2026-11-28)

Rule: do not mirror every Nullborn giant. Starhaven wins by coordination (Soul Link), target choice (Coordinator kills decided Ashfield), doctrine and legends. Covered already: Ogres / Bone Golems / Null Leviathans (Varathos giants, Glacierwalkers, Hoarbacks, Forest Golems, War Bears, mammoths, elephants, Ironframe), the sky (Ninth Sky, Skyhammer, Skyhunter, Aurora Lancers, Iron Talon), Walking Bastions (Iron Tide now; **SKYREACH** is the long answer, §0b). The **Malgrath-Touched stays unmatched on purpose** — the dread is the point; the long-term hint is in §0j (the pre-Sundering flying fortresses were built to hunt the Void).

**Gap 1 — Titans need a named hero.** Titans DO die in canon, but only to legends and strong mages: Kael + Rayden (Ch3 checkpoint), Rayden alone (Ch4, Kingsbane through the skull; and Ch6, knee then eye), Velkan from the drake with Cinderwake (Ch6), **Kai alone** with a stone spike through the throat (Ch6), Thorin + Garruk on two, then Thorin in dire-bear form (Ch6, eight Titans in all), Drogar + Barek Kade (Greypeak 1059). Most of the Eleven and several commanders can kill one, given time. The gap: ordinary soldiers and forts cannot. The four Guardians are once-per-target, the Void Siege Battery needs dual authorization, Cain's twelve Wrought are classified. Eleven-plus Titans at Ashfield, or one at a fort with no Pillar in it, and the answer runs out. Same root as the Council's "too much rests on a few people".
- Shown at: **Ironpass night siege** — the heroes are elsewhere or busy; the first Titan uses up the Guardian release, the next ones walk in.
- Fixed at: **Winter Council** orders **Titanbreaker crews**, so a Titan no longer needs a Pillar: small mixed teams built round one of Cain's Wrought, a Skybreaker lance fired from the ground (Skyhunter tech, Skymark guidance), Stonemark rune-anchors to pin a leg, a Void Breaker to cut the cord (Zareth's Severance doctrine), and an Arcane Cannon from Kai's corps for the killing shot. Tools and drill, not talent: Kai's lone kill in 1042 was Kai — ordinary soldiers cannot match it, so the crew is built to need nobody special. Only started at the Council; first used in the field later (1060–61). Card only once a story uses them.

**Gap 2 — sea monsters (Abyss Leviathans, 9; also Bone Dreadnoughts).** Starhaven has fleets but no answer below the waterline except legends (Aruksha's leap at Forty Sails, Tharos). Geography: the Nullborn Empire and its seas are **south-east** (Nullborn Empire ~840,730; Coral Archipelago ~820,560) — so this belongs to the next **Nullborn sea fight in the south or east**, NOT the Karvossan war (west). Decide when the Silver Isle's position is set (§1).
- Source: the **Reefborn of the Coral Archipelago** (King Taneo Reefcrown) — sea-people with gills and soft lights in their skin, cities of living coral; Starhaven took back their Great Reef in *The King Under the Reef* (1056–57), so they owe the kingdom. They have lived beside the deep for ages, beside the things the Nullborn later chained.
- **[Suggested] their secrets** (pick when the arc is planned): (a) the leviathans' old names and songs — the Reefborn once shared the deep with them, and a chained leviathan may still answer its name; (b) living coral grown onto a hull that seals its own holes, so Bone Dreadnought rib-cannons do not sink it; (c) **Deepwardens** — Reefborn fighters who go into the water with coral-tipped harpoons and lights in their skin that leviathans read as their own kind. Keep one secret back for later.
- Fixed by: a Reefborn alliance story (the king who sent a hundred shells is asked for help in return), then a joint unit — card only when a story uses it.

### Suggested city (1060 onward): Goldwake(open to chaneg name) 

**Site:** The first red-circled area, between Saddlestone Keep and Veilhold Keep, near the Saffron Terraces and the Ashfield road. This spot can connect the farm villages to the frontier keeps and the southern road. Leave the second marked area open for a different future settlement.

**Origin:** In 1060, a Crown survey crew begins laying an all-weather route between the frontier posts. A cut into a low ridge opens a sealed gate and reveals the upper streets of a city buried centuries ago. Its old aqueduct failed after an earthquake shifted the underground watercourse; the city was abandoned, not Void-corrupted. Surveyors then find a modest but rich gold-bearing seam beneath its old mint district.

**Why rebuild it:** Goldwake gives Starhaven a second domestic gold source after the Winter Council identifies its reliance on the Sable Gold Concession. The find is valuable enough to support a city, but not so large that it replaces Sorvane's concession or turns the kingdom into a gold rush. The old stone roads, cisterns and sheltered foundations make rebuilding practical; nearby orchards, vineyards and saffron farms can feed a growing market town.

**How it forms:** 1060 begins as a guarded survey camp and a careful archaeological dig, not a city built overnight. A public charter reserves the old quarter and aqueduct for restoration, permits staged mining under the Worker Rights Compact, and directs a share of the gold revenue to the road, clinic, granary and schools. Miners, masons, merchants and farm families settle around the recovered gate. Over the next several years, the camp grows into a chartered city with a market square above the old mint and new homes built around—not on top of—the exposed ruins.

**Story hook:** Mining deeper threatens the aqueduct that keeps the surrounding farms alive. The city must decide whether to chase the richest vein or preserve its water and the old city's remaining districts. Working name **Goldwake**; alternatives: **Gildhollow** or **Aurelfall**.

### Water resilience: Winter Council → Goldwake(open to chaneg name) [agreed direction, planning only]

- Treat water security as a separate weakness from the single Ashen river route, which is a transport vulnerability. The Council commissions a survey to map groundwater and identify how much Starhaven relies on a few known river sources.
- An Arcanum water mage with a water-finding ability joins the survey. The ability points to promising sources; engineers still test safety, quality and replenishment before anyone relies on a well.
- The Crown recruits the proposed male field archaeologist Ronan Haldvik from the Frostspire Dominion for a permanent research post. He has mapped ice-sealed archive passages and buried underworks; his remit covers hidden underground structures, sealed spaces, buried routes, relics and physical evidence connected to Void history. He works closely with Velkan, bringing field evidence and material context to Velkan's arcane research; he is not himself a mage. A joint scholarly appointment and Starhaven's preservation charter give him reason to make the move. His work is not limited to this survey or one arc.
- Goldwake can be one early field project in 1060, not the reason for creating or recruiting him. The water-finder helps locate the buried watercourse; Ronan leads the careful excavation after the road crew opens the sealed gate. The restored aqueduct and farm supply must be protected from the gold mine. His wider work continues across underground and Void-related discoveries in multiple regions.

### §0e. The Winter Council's lighter side, and the Wedding (agreed 2026-11-24)

- **Winter Council, comic beat:** when the table reaches the king's own weak point (succession), ministers start telling Rayden — politely, then less politely — to have children. In the Black Charge, a rider tells Vespera; Vespera goes red to the ears and bonks the rider on the head.
