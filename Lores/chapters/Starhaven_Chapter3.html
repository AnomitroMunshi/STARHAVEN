I. Ashes of Veynehold

Sundered Age — Year 1039

The fires of Veynehold still stained the eastern horizon two nights later.

No one celebrated the victory.

Not the rescued prisoners.

Not the wounded refugees.

Not even the soldiers who survived the night.

Behind them, Veynehold still burned beneath the frozen dawn.

Black smoke climbed endlessly into the sky while shattered fortress towers collapsed one after another deep within the distant snowstorm.

The city no longer looked conquered.

It looked condemned.

Rayden walked at the rear of the refugee column alone.

Kingsbane rested across his shoulder.

The giant black-and-gold execution blade still carried dried blood along its edge while Vossfang’s chain occasionally rattled softly against his armored forearm during the march.

The small wooden sword remained tucked quietly beneath his cloak.

People noticed him constantly.

But nobody approached.

Whispers still moved through the refugee line carefully whenever he passed.

“The Kingsbane.”

“The Butcher of Veynehold.”

“He killed the executioner barehanded.”

“I saw him tear that corrupted monster apart...”

Rayden ignored all of it.

His body hurt.

Berserker always left damage afterward.

Every muscle felt torn apart beneath his armor while overusing Temporal Frenzy still left pressure behind his eyes like fractures spreading through his skull.

But physical pain barely reached him anymore.

Selene should have been there.

She would have already moved through the refugee line checking wounds.

Comforting frightened children.

Arguing with stubborn soldiers refusing treatment.

Keeping people alive.

Instead only silence followed him now.

The refugee column eventually stopped beside the ruins of an abandoned southern watchpost several miles away from Veynehold.

Small fires were lit against the freezing wind.

Exhausted civilians collapsed beside broken carts.

Children finally slept.

Rayden stood watch alone near the outer ridge overlooking the frozen valley.

Then something felt wrong.

His eyes scanned the camp again slowly.

The brother and sister.

Missing.

Rayden’s expression darkened immediately.

Without saying a word to anyone, he turned back toward the burning ruins of Veynehold.

Snowfall thickened around him as he crossed through the shattered southern district alone.

The city had become almost unrecognizable now.

Collapsed homes.

Frozen corpses.

Burning streets buried beneath drifting ash.

The silence after battle somehow felt worse than the fighting itself.

Then Rayden saw light ahead.

A weak fire burned beneath the remains of a collapsed stone archway.

Several injured civilians huddled beside it wrapped in scavenged blankets.

And standing beside them...

was the boy.

Dawnpiercer’s shield rested planted into the frozen ground before the survivors while the boy sat awake beside it gripping the black-and-gold sword with both hands.

The blade still looked far too large for him.

His arms trembled visibly from exhaustion.

Fear still lingered inside his eyes.

But he stayed anyway.

The little sister slept curled safely behind the shield.

Rayden stopped walking.

For a brief moment...

the ruined street no longer looked like Veynehold.

It looked like Oaksmane.

Garrick standing between frightened children and the world.

The boy finally noticed Rayden approaching through the snowfall.

He immediately forced himself back to his feet despite exhaustion.

Still protecting the others first.

Even now.

Neither of them spoke for several long seconds.

The fire crackled softly between drifting snow.

Then the boy carefully lifted Dawnpiercer using both hands and offered it back.

“I tried to use it properly.”

Rayden stared at him silently.

Then slowly took hold of the sword again.

“You already did.”

The boy looked confused by the answer.

Rayden secured Dawnpiercer’s shield onto his back beside Kingsbane once more.

The curved saber returned quietly to his waist where it belonged.

For the first time since entering Veynehold...

the protector and the executioner were carried by the same man again.

One blade built to protect.

One built to execute.

Both carried by the same man.

The little sister stirred awake behind the shield sleepily.

When she saw Rayden standing there, she smiled faintly before falling asleep again almost immediately.

Something inside Rayden hurt unexpectedly after seeing that.

Not physically.

His eyes slowly lifted eastward afterward.

Beyond Veynehold.

Beyond the ruined frontier.

The corruption symbols beneath Veynehold matched markings discovered months earlier near Blackwall.

And farther east...

the reports continued.

Villages disappearing.

Roads going silent.

Black growths spreading beneath frozen rivers.

This was not ending.

It was spreading.

Rayden looked once more toward the frightened survivors gathered around the weak fire.

Then toward the eastern darkness beyond the mountains.

Somewhere deep inside himself...

he already knew he would keep walking toward it.

Even if the road eventually killed him.

II — The Eastward Trail

Sundered Age — Year 1039

Five days after Veynehold’s fall, Rayden Cale disappeared from the refugee camps before sunrise.

No speeches.

No farewells.

No promises to return.

Only footprints vanishing eastward through fresh snow.

The frontier roads beyond Veynehold felt abandoned by civilization itself.

Broken wagons remained frozen beside collapsed pathways while burned military checkpoints stood empty beneath drifting ash.

Even the crows had mostly disappeared.

Rayden traveled alone.

Kingsbane rested diagonally across his back.

Dawnpiercer’s round shield remained secured beside it while the curved saber hung quietly at his waist once more.

Vossfang wrapped around his forearm like a sleeping predator beneath black leather bindings.

And hidden carefully beneath his cloak...

the small wooden sword remained untouched.

Snow crunched softly beneath his boots as he crossed the dead frontier roads.

Everywhere he looked...

the world seemed tired.

Not destroyed.

Worse.

Exhausted.

Villages stood half-abandoned behind barricaded doors.

Fields rotted beneath winter frost because nobody remained alive enough to harvest them.

Watchtowers flew kingdom banners above empty walls guarded by starving soldiers too afraid to patrol beyond torchlight.

And everywhere...

people whispered about the corruption.

Black rivers.

Missing caravans.

Livestock born twisted.

Entire roads swallowed by unnatural fog.

Rayden listened carefully.

Then kept walking east.

The farther he traveled, the more frequently he began finding them.

The marks.

Black crystalline growths spreading beneath frozen stone.

The same fractures from Veynehold.

The same symbols carved beneath Blackwall months earlier.

Connected.

That realization followed him constantly now.

This was not random corruption.

Not isolated outbreaks.

Something was spreading deliberately across the continent.

On the seventh night of travel, Rayden reached what remained of an eastern supply route once maintained by frontier kingdoms before the corruption swallowed the region.

The road had gone silent.

Too silent.

Rayden crouched slowly beside frozen wagon tracks buried beneath snowfall.

Then his eyes narrowed.

The tracks stopped abruptly.

No struggle.

No blood.

No broken wheels.

The caravan had simply...

vanished.

Cold wind moved softly through dead pine trees surrounding the road.

Then Rayden noticed something else.

Footprints.

Fresh.

Not civilian.

Military.

Heavy boots.

Disciplined spacing.

Someone experienced.

Rayden followed the trail uphill through the frozen forest cautiously.

His hand rested near Dawnpiercer instinctively while Vossfang’s chain shifted quietly beneath his forearm.

The forest gradually darkened around him.

Even the snowfall sounded muted there.

Then he found the bodies.

Six corrupted creatures.

Dead.

Not recently.

But not old either.

Rayden examined them silently.

The wounds immediately felt wrong.

Precise.

Every kill strike landed perfectly through vital points.

No wasted movement.

No panic.

No excessive brutality.

Professional.

Rayden’s eyes narrowed further.

Then he noticed the final detail.

One corrupted corpse had been pinned directly into a frozen tree trunk by a black throwing blade marked with unfamiliar silver engravings.

Not frontier craftsmanship.

Someone else was hunting the corruption too.

And unlike frightened soldiers or desperate mercenaries...

this person knew exactly what they were doing.

Snowfall drifted quietly through the dead forest while Rayden slowly rose back to his feet.

For the first time since leaving Veynehold...

he no longer felt alone on the eastern roads.

III — Roads of the Dying Kingdoms

Sundered Age — Year 1039-1040

Three days later, Rayden reached the old Frostmere trade road.

The kingdom checkpoint guarding the pass had long since collapsed.

Broken barricades remained half-buried beneath snow while rusted warning bells swayed quietly in the freezing wind.

No guards.

No patrols.

No kingdom authority left.

Only the desperate survived on roads like these now.

Rayden noticed the wagon first.

One broken wheel.

Frozen horse carcass beside the ditch.

Smoke from a weak campfire barely surviving the wind.

Then the voices.

“You still owe us.”

“We protected this road, didn’t we?”

“Maybe your daughter can pay instead.”

Rayden’s eyes narrowed.

Five bandits surrounded the stranded refugee family near the broken wagon.

The father already lay bleeding in the snow beside them clutching his ribs while the mother desperately held two frightened children behind her.

The bandits looked starving.

Desperate.

Not soldiers.

Not monsters.

Just another symptom of a dying continent.

One of them noticed Rayden approaching through the snowfall.

The man froze immediately after seeing Kingsbane.

“Wait...”

“That’s him.”

The others turned nervously.

Even starving criminals had heard stories already.

The black-and-gold warrior.

The Kingsbane Wraith.

Veynehold’s butcher.

Rayden stopped several feet away.

Silent.

Snow drifted softly around him while Kingsbane remained strapped across his back untouched.

“Leave.”

The leader tried hiding his fear.

Tried.

“We found them first.”

“Road taxes.”

Rayden’s expression never changed.

“Last warning.”

Tension tightened instantly.

The youngest bandit panicked first.

He lunged with a knife.

Huge mistake.

Rayden moved before the blade even finished extending.

His hand caught the attacker’s wrist mid-strike.

Then twisted.

The elbow bent backward unnaturally with a wet snap.

The bandit screamed.

Rayden stepped inward immediately.

One brutal knee strike crashed directly into the man’s stomach hard enough to lift him partially off the ground.

Air exploded from his lungs.

The second bandit charged from the side swinging a rusted axe wildly.

Rayden pivoted.

Caught the attacker by the shoulder.

Used the momentum.

Then threw him violently headfirst into the wagon wheel.

Wood shattered.

Teeth scattered across the snow.

The mother screamed while shielding the children behind her.

Rayden instinctively shifted position between them and the remaining attackers without even realizing it.

The third bandit drew a short sword shakily.

Desperate now.

Rayden closed distance instantly.

The man barely managed half a swing before Rayden crashed into him chest-first like a battering ram.

They hit the frozen ground together.

Rayden trapped the sword arm under his knee.

Then drove repeated punches directly into the man’s jaw with horrifying efficiency.

One.

Two.

Three.

The body stopped moving.

The final two broke immediately.

Fear finally overwhelmed hunger.

One tried running.

Vossfang launched instantly.

The chain wrapped around the man’s ankle mid-stride.

Rayden yanked once.

The bandit slammed backward into the frozen road hard enough to crack stone beneath his skull.

The last attacker grabbed the terrified little girl desperately.

Knife against her throat.

“STAY BACK!”

Everything went still.

Snow drifted silently between them.

Rayden stared at the trembling man for several long seconds.

Then slowly raised both hands away from his weapons.

The bandit relaxed slightly.

Another mistake.

Rayden exploded forward.

Too fast.

He trapped the knife arm.

Rotated underneath it.

Locked the shoulder.

Then slammed the man face-first into the wagon hard enough to collapse the entire side panel inward.

The little girl stumbled free crying.

Rayden caught her before she hit the ground.

Silence returned to the road afterward.

The surviving bandits crawled away through the snow dragging broken bodies behind them.

None dared look back.

Rayden finally released a slow breath.

His knuckles bled.

Kingsbane remained untouched.

The injured father stared upward at him in disbelief.

“You... you didn’t even draw your sword.”

Rayden said nothing.

Instead he crouched beside the broken wagon wheel silently.

Then began repairing it himself.

The little girl watched him nervously for several minutes from behind her mother before finally whispering:

“...Are you a knight?”

Rayden’s hands stopped briefly against the frozen wood.

For several seconds he genuinely did not know the answer.

The family remained strangely quiet while Rayden repaired the wagon.

Almost cautious around him.

The mother cleaned blood from the little girl’s face while the injured father watched Rayden work beside the broken wheel with visible confusion.

Because nothing about the black-and-gold warrior made sense.

The weapons on his back belonged to battlefields.

To executions.

To war.

Yet the same blood-covered hands that had nearly beaten a man to death moments earlier now carefully repaired cracked wood beneath falling snow.

The contradiction unsettled them more than the violence had.

Rayden finally tightened the final support pin back into place.

The wagon wheel held again.

“You should leave this road before nightfall.”

The father pushed himself upright painfully against the wagon.

“We were trying to reach Dunmere.”

“They said the eastern towns still had food.”

Rayden’s expression darkened slightly.

He had passed through Dunmere two days earlier.

Half the town had already emptied.

The remaining civilians looked one bad winter away from killing each other over bread.

But he did not say that aloud.

The little girl stepped closer afterward holding a torn wool blanket around herself.

She stared openly at Dawnpiercer’s shield resting behind Rayden’s shoulder.

“It’s pretty.”

Rayden looked back slightly.

Most people called Kingsbane terrifying.

Very few noticed Dawnpiercer first.

“My brother says only real knights carry shields.”

Something inside Rayden tightened again unexpectedly.

Garrick used to say the same thing.

“A shield tells people what you protect.”

The memory arrived so suddenly it almost hurt physically.

Rayden stood slowly afterward.

Snow continued drifting across the ruined trade road around them.

Then he noticed movement.

Not far away.

Tree line.

Rayden’s eyes narrowed instantly.

Someone was watching.

The presence vanished almost immediately once noticed.

Fast.

Professional.

Rayden turned toward the forest silently.

His hand rested instinctively near Dawnpiercer’s hilt.

No sound followed afterward.

No attack.

Only drifting snowfall through dead trees.

But the feeling remained.

Someone had absolutely been there.

And whoever it was...

had watched the entire fight.

Rayden looked once more toward the frightened family before finally turning eastward again.

The road ahead disappeared into frozen mountains and dying kingdoms beyond.

Then without another word...

the black-and-gold warrior continued walking.

Far behind him, the little girl suddenly tugged lightly on her mother’s sleeve.

“He wasn’t scary.”

The mother looked toward Rayden’s distant silhouette disappearing through the snowfall.

Kingsbane across his back.

Dawnpiercer’s shield behind his shoulder.

A walking contradiction between protector and executioner.

Then quietly:

“No...”

“He looked tired.”

IV — The Man Called Kael Varric

Sundered Age — Year 1040

The eastern roads became dead quieter the farther Rayden traveled.

Villages disappeared completely now. Not abandoned — erased. Burned homes. Frozen blood beneath snow. Watchtowers standing empty with kingdom banners still moving in the wind above corpses no one buried anymore.

And everywhere...

the corruption spread.

Black veins beneath river ice. Dead animals twisted unnaturally into shapes no creature should possess. Entire forests silent except for distant whispers moving between trees after dark.

Rayden kept following the trail east.

And repeatedly, he kept finding the same thing.

Someone was already here before him.

Corrupted nests destroyed with military precision. Civilians evacuated before attacks reached them. Barricades reinforced correctly. Supply caches left behind intentionally for refugees.

No wasted movement.

No chaos.

Disciplined.

That bothered Rayden more than the corruption did.

On the tenth night after leaving Veynehold, he reached the ruins of an old mountain checkpoint overlooking the Black Hollow Pass.

Smoke rose ahead.

Fresh.

Rayden moved immediately.

The checkpoint had become a battlefield.

Corrupted soldiers slammed violently against broken barricades while terrified civilians hid behind overturned supply wagons near the inner gate.

And standing between them...

was a man carrying a tower shield.

Kael Varric.

Even exhausted, the stranger moved like a veteran fortress commander.

No unnecessary motion.

No panic.

No wasted strikes.

One corrupted soldier lunged.

Kael stepped sideways minimally.

The tower shield redirected the creature’s momentum before a brutal shield-edge strike crushed its throat instantly.

Another attacked from behind.

Kael pivoted.

Short sword through the eye.

Efficient.

Cold.

Rayden watched silently for several seconds.

Then the barricade collapsed.

Three corrupted creatures surged through toward the civilians.

Rayden moved instantly.

Vossfang launched upward while Kingsbane came free from his back in the same motion.

The first creature disappeared beneath the impact entirely.

The second lost half its torso.

The third reached the civilians before Rayden physically intercepted it mid-charge and drove it headfirst through a stone support column hard enough to crack the entire wall.

Kael noticed him immediately.

And immediately disliked what he saw.

Too brutal.

Too aggressive.

Too familiar.

He had seen warriors fight like that before.

Most of them eventually stopped being human.

The corruption wave intensified suddenly.

More creatures climbed through the shattered checkpoint walls while black growths spread visibly beneath frozen stone.

Rayden activated Temporal Frenzy.

The world slowed.

Snow froze motionless around him while black-and-gold energy pulsed beneath fractured armor plates.

Kael’s expression hardened instantly.

Not normal.

Rayden crossed the battlefield like a moving execution.

Kingsbane tore through corrupted bodies while Vossfang’s chains redirected enemies away from civilians repeatedly.

Every movement looked controlled.

But barely.

Then Berserker activated.

The transformation erupted violently through the checkpoint.

Rayden caught one corrupted soldier by the throat and slammed it through the frozen ground hard enough to crater stone beneath them before ripping Kingsbane upward through another creature’s ribcage in the same movement.

Kael stared.

And for one dangerous moment...

he genuinely thought the corruption had already reached him.

The final creature collapsed moments later.

Silence returned across the ruined checkpoint.

Heavy breathing. Falling snow. Blood freezing across broken stone.

Rayden slowly turned toward the civilians to check survivors.

Kael noticed it immediately.

Even after Berserker...

his first instinct was still protection.

But Kael did not lower his shield.

“What exactly are you?”

Rayden’s eyes narrowed.

“Tired.”

Kael stepped forward anyway.

Tower shield lowered into combat stance.

“I’ve seen corruption wear human faces before.”

“I won’t watch another fortress fall because I hesitated.”

Rayden’s expression darkened immediately.

He was exhausted of that look already.

The fear.

The suspicion.

Kingsbane lowered slightly.

Not fully.

“You want to test that theory?”

Kael answered by moving first.

The tower shield crashed forward like a battering ram.

Rayden twisted sideways narrowly before countering with a brutal elbow strike toward Kael’s jaw.

Kael blocked it with the shield rim and drove his shoulder directly into Rayden’s chest hard enough to push him backward through snow.

Rayden responded instantly.

Vossfang launched.

The chain wrapped around Kael’s shield before Rayden used the momentum to pull himself forward into a knee strike.

Kael absorbed it.

Barely.

The veteran immediately trapped Rayden’s arm, rotated his stance, and attempted to throw him directly into the ruined barricade.

But Rayden planted one boot into frozen stone and stopped the movement halfway through using raw strength.

For several seconds both men remained locked there physically.

Power against structure.

Aggression against discipline.

Then screaming interrupted them.

A hidden corrupted creature burst upward from beneath the checkpoint ruins directly toward the surviving civilians.

Both men moved instantly.

Together.

Rayden reached the civilians first.

Not the enemy.

He physically positioned himself between the creature and the frightened survivors before Kingsbane even moved.

Kael saw that.

Really saw it.

The corruption creature died seconds later beneath combined steel and shield strikes.

Silence returned once more.

Snow drifted quietly across the ruined checkpoint.

Kael slowly lowered his shield.

Not fully trusting.

But no longer preparing to kill him.

“You hit like a collapsing fortress.”

Rayden wiped blood from his mouth with the back of his hand.

“You block like one.”

For the first time since leaving Veynehold...

someone almost smiled.

The civilians were escorted inside the ruined checkpoint shortly afterward.

Small fires were lit beneath broken stone archways while the wounded received what little treatment remained available.

Rayden sat alone near the outer wall sharpening Dawnpiercer’s curved saber quietly against a whetstone.

Unlike Kingsbane, the weapon felt lighter.

Cleaner.

Built for precision instead of devastation.

Kael watched him from across the ruined courtyard without speaking initially.

The younger warrior’s equipment told conflicting stories.

Kingsbane looked forged for execution.

Dawnpiercer looked forged for protection.

And Vossfang...

looked like something designed by a man expecting battlefields to collapse around him constantly.

None of it fit together naturally.

Neither did Rayden.

Eventually Kael approached carrying a small iron kettle filled with heated snow water.

He set it beside the fire between them.

“You fight like someone trying to outrun death.”

Rayden kept sharpening the saber.

“Usually works.”

Kael’s eyes shifted briefly toward Kingsbane resting nearby against the ruined wall.

The massive black-and-gold greatsword looked monstrous beside the smaller Dawnpiercer saber.

“That thing wasn’t forged for humans.”

“No.”

Rayden finally secured Dawnpiercer back into its sheath at his waist afterward.

Then leaned slightly against the ruined stone behind him.

“You always attack people who help you?”

Kael gave the faintest shrug.

“I attack corruption before it spreads.”

That answer carried no arrogance.

Only exhaustion.

Rayden noticed it immediately.

The older man’s armor carried too many repaired fractures. His shield edges were worn down from years of impacts. Even his stance looked tired when he stopped moving.

This was not a knight chasing glory.

This was a survivor.

Wind moved softly through the broken checkpoint while snow drifted beyond shattered walls.

For several minutes neither man spoke.

Then Kael finally looked toward Dawnpiercer’s shield secured beside Kingsbane.

“You carry both because you haven’t decided what you are yet.”

Rayden’s eyes narrowed slightly.

Kael continued calmly.

“One weapon protects people.”

“The other kills anything threatening them.”

“You’re trying to convince yourself those two things can coexist.”

Silence followed.

Because the older warrior was uncomfortably correct.

Rayden looked toward the civilians sleeping behind the ruined barricades.

Children wrapped in scavenged blankets.

Wounded refugees finally resting safely.

Then quietly:

“They have to coexist.”

Kael studied him carefully after that.

Not the weapons.

Not the rumors.

The man underneath them.

Far beyond the checkpoint walls, somewhere deep in the eastern mountains...

a distant corrupted roar echoed through the frozen night.

Neither warrior looked surprised.

Because both already knew.

The road east was only going to get worse.

The storm worsened near midnight.

Black clouds swallowed the mountain sky while freezing wind howled through the ruined checkpoint hard enough to shake weakened stone walls.

Most of the civilians slept anyway.

Exhaustion eventually overpowered fear.

Kael remained awake beside the outer barricade reinforcing damaged supports with scavenged timber.

Rayden noticed immediately.

The man never stopped working.

Even after battle.

Even wounded.

Rayden eventually stood and walked over silently.

Dawnpiercer’s shield shifted softly against Kingsbane across his back while Vossfang’s chain rattled faintly beneath his forearm.

Without speaking, he grabbed one of the collapsed support beams himself and lifted it back into place.

Kael glanced sideways briefly.

“You always help rebuild places after destroying them?”

Rayden secured the beam into the frozen stone.

“Only the ones worth rebuilding.”

That answer lingered quietly between them.

Kael hammered iron spikes through the timber supports afterward while Rayden anchored damaged barricades using Vossfang’s chains to hold unstable sections together temporarily.

The bracer’s black-and-gold chains tightened across fractured stone with metallic groans.

For the first time since meeting...

they worked beside each other instead of against each other.

The checkpoint slowly became defensible again.

One of the civilians approached nervously sometime later carrying a small bowl of watered stew.

An older woman.

Thin enough to look half-starved already.

She offered the bowl toward Rayden first.

Then hesitated after seeing Kingsbane nearby.

Rayden noticed.

He always noticed now.

Instead of taking the bowl himself, he handed it toward one of the sleeping children nearby.

The woman’s expression softened slightly afterward.

Kael watched the exchange silently.

Another contradiction.

Later, while reinforcing the final barricade section, Kael finally spoke again.

“Where did you learn to fight like that?”

Rayden understood immediately he was not asking about Kingsbane.

He meant the hand-to-hand fighting.

“Mercenary pits.”

“Frontier wars.”

“Anywhere people tried killing me.”

Kael nodded once.

No judgment.

Only recognition.

“You grapple like Iron Legion veterans.”

Rayden looked toward him slightly.

“You know them?”

Kael’s expression darkened faintly.

“I buried most of them.”

Silence returned immediately afterward.

Heavy.

Somewhere beyond the ruined walls, corrupted creatures howled faintly within the mountains.

Closer this time.

Rayden instinctively rested one hand against Dawnpiercer’s hilt.

Kael noticed that too.

Not Kingsbane.

Dawnpiercer.

The protector first.

That mattered.

Kael finally leaned back against the reinforced barricade and folded his arms.

“You know what the eastern kingdoms are saying about you?”

Rayden looked unimpressed.

“Depends which kingdom.”

“Some think you’re a hero.”

“Most think you’re a monster.”

“One frontier lord offered five thousand gold for proof of your death.”

Rayden almost smiled faintly at that.

Almost.

“Too low.”

Kael stared at him for several seconds afterward.

Then unexpectedly...

a short rough laugh escaped him.

Not warm.

Not joyful.

But real.

And for the first time since Selene died...

Rayden did not feel entirely alone beside the fire.

The fire burned lower as the night deepened.

Snow continued falling beyond the ruined checkpoint walls while distant corruption cries echoed faintly through the eastern mountains.

Most of the civilians finally slept peacefully now.

For the first time in days, nobody expected executioners, monsters, or starvation to reach them before sunrise.

Kael noticed something unusual afterward.

Rayden never fully rested.

Even sitting beside the fire, one hand remained near Dawnpiercer’s hilt while Vossfang’s chains rested loose around his forearm ready to launch instantly.

Kingsbane leaned nearby within arm’s reach.

The man slept like someone expecting war to arrive at any second.

Kael understood that feeling too well.

Eventually Rayden spoke quietly without opening his eyes.

“You’ve been following me since Frostmere Road.”

Kael did not look surprised he noticed.

“You noticed late.”

Rayden opened one eye slightly.

“You hide well.”

“You make too much noise.”

That almost became another smile.

Almost.

Wind howled through the broken checkpoint again.

The weakened walls groaned loudly.

Then Kael’s expression slowly hardened.

“You lost someone recently.”

Not a question.

Rayden’s face became unreadable immediately.

Kael stared quietly into the fire afterward.

“Men who fight like you usually have two choices.”

“They either die quickly...”

“...or they survive long enough to become empty.”

Silence followed.

Heavy.

Rayden looked down toward the firelight reflecting across Dawnpiercer’s black-and-gold guard.

Then toward Kingsbane resting in shadow beside it.

“I’m still deciding.”

Kael studied him carefully after that.

The younger warrior looked exhausted beyond physical fatigue now.

Like someone carrying grief heavy enough to fracture bone.

And yet...

he still stopped for civilians.

Still rebuilt walls.

Still protected children first.

That contradicted everything Kael expected from men consumed by violence.

Before either man could speak again...

Vossfang’s chains suddenly tightened sharply against Rayden’s forearm.

Instantly both warriors stood.

Something moved outside the walls.

Fast.

The civilians woke immediately as low growls echoed through the storm beyond the checkpoint.

Kael grabbed his shield.

Rayden drew Dawnpiercer instead of Kingsbane this time.

The curved black-and-gold saber reflected the firelight sharply while the round shield locked into place against his forearm in one smooth movement.

Kael noticed immediately.

Different stance.

Different balance.

More controlled.

Not the executioner.

The protector.

Dark figures slowly emerged beyond the snowfall afterward.

Not corrupted.

Refugees.

A dozen of them.

Half-frozen.

Starving.

Carrying children through the storm.

The lead survivor collapsed before even reaching the gate.

“Please...”

“They’re behind us...”

Then somewhere deep within the mountains beyond the storm...

something enormous roared.

The sound shook snow loose from the ruined walls themselves.

Kael slowly lifted his shield.

Rayden tightened his grip on Dawnpiercer.

And somewhere beyond the darkness ahead...

something was coming toward the checkpoint.

The roar echoed again.

Closer now.

The ruined checkpoint fell completely silent except for the storm.

Even the exhausted refugees stopped moving.

Kael stepped toward the outer barricade first, shield raised.

Rayden moved beside him with Dawnpiercer drawn in one hand while the round shield locked against his forearm.

Kingsbane remained behind him for now.

The distinction mattered.

Whatever approached...

his first instinct was still defense.

Snow drifted violently through the broken gate as the refugees were hurried inside.

Several children were barely conscious from cold.

One woman carried a wounded boy wrapped in blood-soaked cloth.

Kael immediately started positioning civilians behind reinforced walls.

Efficient.

Calm.

Practiced.

“Lanterns low.”

“Keep everyone away from the eastern breach.”

“If the walls fall, run west immediately.”

The civilians obeyed without hesitation.

Kael spoke like someone used to command long before this checkpoint existed.

Rayden noticed black blood across the newest refugee’s boots.

Fresh.

“What followed you?”

The wounded man struggled to breathe.

“Not soldiers...”

The convoy finally crossed the collapsed pass moments later and entered a narrow valley surrounded by towering black cliffs.

Dead trees stretched across the landscape like skeletal hands beneath the storm.

Then the fog moved.

Not wind.

Something inside it.

A massive corrupted beast erupted from the treeline without warning.

Not a Siegeborn Titan.

Smaller.

Faster.

Built to hunt.

The creature slammed directly into the convoy’s front wagons, overturning wood and civilians into the mud while black rain exploded around it.

Blue corruption burned beneath stretched muscle across its body.

Its skull looked almost wolf-like beneath layers of blackened armor growths.

Children screamed.

Kael reacted instantly.

The tower shield crashed forward into the creature’s side hard enough to redirect its momentum away from the civilians.

The beast skidded violently through mud and stone.

But unlike normal corruption...

it recovered immediately.

The creature lunged back toward Kael with terrifying speed.

Kael absorbed the impact directly.

The tower shield held.

But the sheer force drove him backward through the mud several feet.

Rayden saw the problem immediately.

Too close to civilians for Kingsbane.

The beast moved too unpredictably.

Dawnpiercer remained in his hand.

Rayden launched forward.

Vossfang anchored overhead while he accelerated through the storm at impossible speed.

Dawnpiercer’s shield smashed directly across the beast’s skull from the side.

The impact stunned the creature for half a second.

Enough.

The curved saber pierced deep into the creature’s shoulder joint while Rayden used the shield edge to redirect its claws away from Kael simultaneously.

Precise.

Controlled.

Efficient.

The beast roared violently and spun toward Rayden instead.

Its claws tore through the storm fast enough to shred stone.

Rayden ducked beneath the strike.

The shield hooked underneath the creature’s forelimb while Vossfang launched around a nearby dead tree.

Then Rayden pulled.

The chain tension twisted the beast sideways violently off balance.

Kael understood immediately.

The older warrior stepped forward with terrifying calm while drawing the warhammer once more.

Not rushed.

Not aggressive.

Inevitable.

The corrupted beast recovered just in time to look upward.

Kael’s warhammer descended.

The impact shattered the creature’s front shoulder completely.

Bone exploded outward across black rain and mud.

The beast screamed.

Rayden moved instantly afterward.

Dawnpiercer’s shield slammed across the creature’s skull again while the saber pierced through the exposed throat beneath the jaw.

The corrupted beast collapsed into the mud twitching violently.

But the black rain kept falling.

And the corpse immediately began convulsing again.

“Back away!”

Kael shouted.

Blue corruption spread rapidly beneath the dead creature’s flesh while black crystal growths erupted from the wound sites unnaturally fast.

Rayden’s expression hardened.

Now Kingsbane came free.

One strike.

The massive execution blade obliterated the corrupted corpse completely before reanimation could finish.

Black fire and shattered flesh scattered across the valley floor.

Silence followed.

Only heavy breathing and black rain remained.

The civilians stared at both warriors afterward.

Not just fearful now.

Hopeful.

Because for the first time since the eastern collapse began...

someone was actually holding the darkness back.

The convoy did not stop after the beast died.

No one trusted the black rain anymore.

Not the corpses.

Not the silence.

Not even the ground beneath their feet.

Kael kept everyone moving through the valley while civilians dragged damaged wagons through mud blackened by corruption.

Several refugees were visibly weakening now.

The rain drained people.

Rayden walked near the rear formation afterward.

Dawnpiercer rested unsheathed across his shoulder while the round shield remained secured against his forearm.

Kingsbane stayed on his back once more.

Back to control.

The younger children had slowly stopped fearing him now.

Several actually moved closer whenever the storm intensified.

Instinct.

Predators scared people.

Protectors gathered them.

Rayden still had not noticed the difference yet.

The valley narrowed further as darkness approached.

Jagged cliffs surrounded both sides while black fog drifted unnaturally across the lower ground.

Then Kael suddenly raised one hand.

The convoy stopped immediately.

Rayden moved beside him silently.

Both warriors stared ahead through the storm.

Ruins.

An old mountain monastery built directly into the cliffside itself.

Half-collapsed towers overlooked the valley while broken statues stood buried beneath blackened snow.

Ancient.

Forgotten.

But defensible.

Kael studied the structure carefully.

“We shelter here tonight.”

No one argued.

The refugees moved quickly toward the monastery entrance while Kael immediately organized barricades using collapsed stone and broken wagon remains.

Even exhausted, the older warrior never stopped preparing for the next attack.

Rayden explored deeper inside the ruins afterward.

Dawnpiercer remained drawn.

The monastery felt wrong.

Not corrupted.

Empty.

Old prayer symbols covered the walls beneath centuries of dust and moisture damage.

Most had been scratched apart violently long ago.

Then Rayden noticed something strange.

No bodies.

An abandoned monastery this large should have contained remains.

Instead...

everything looked evacuated in a hurry.

Vossfang tightened faintly.

Rayden’s eyes narrowed.

Something had happened here.

Behind him, footsteps approached slowly.

Kael.

The older warrior looked around the ruined chamber carefully.

“You feel it too.”

Not a question.

Rayden nodded once.

“No bodies.”

Kael’s expression darkened.

“Monks here used to protect refugee roads decades ago.”

“Then one winter...”

“they vanished.”

Wind howled softly through broken monastery windows while black rain continued striking ancient stone outside.

Then suddenly—

a child cried out from the lower chambers.

Both warriors moved instantly.

They reached the main hall seconds later.

Several refugees had gathered around one of the younger boys near the fire.

The child was shivering violently.

Black veins slowly spread beneath the skin across his arm.

Corruption infection.

The civilians panicked immediately.

Several backed away in fear.

Kael’s jaw tightened.

He had seen what happened next before.

Rayden crouched beside the child silently.

The boy looked barely conscious beneath fever and black rain exposure.

“Please...”

“don’t let me become one of them...”

The hall fell completely silent.

Kael slowly lowered his gaze.

Because sometimes...

there was no saving people from corruption.

But Rayden did not move away.

Instead he removed one glove slowly and placed his hand against the boy’s forehead.

The black veins pulsed violently beneath the skin.

Hot.

Wrong.

Then something unexpected happened.

Dawnpiercer began glowing faintly.

The faint golden light spread slowly across Dawnpiercer’s blade.

Not bright.

Not explosive.

Warm.

The refugees stared silently while the curved black-and-gold saber hummed softly beside the feverish child.

The black veins beneath the boy’s skin suddenly recoiled.

Rayden noticed immediately.

So did Kael.

“What is that?”

Rayden frowned slightly.

“I don’t know.”

And for once...

he truly didn’t.

Dawnpiercer’s glow intensified faintly while the corruption beneath the child’s arm slowed its spread.

Not cured.

Contained.

The boy’s breathing gradually steadied afterward.

The black veins stopped climbing farther toward his chest.

Silence filled the monastery hall.

The civilians looked at Rayden differently now.

Again.

Not just fear.

Not just survival.

Something closer to belief.

Rayden hated that look immediately.

Because belief meant expectation.

Expectation meant failure eventually hurt people.

So he stood abruptly.

Dawnpiercer’s glow faded almost instantly afterward.

“Keep him warm.”

Then he walked away before anyone could ask questions.

Kael watched him leave silently.

The older warrior’s expression had become thoughtful now.

Outside the monastery, black rain still hammered the mountains endlessly.

Rayden stood alone beneath the ruined archways overlooking the valley below.

Dawnpiercer rested across his knees while Kingsbane remained planted beside him against the stone.

For several long minutes he simply stared at the saber.

The weapon had reacted before.

Near corruption.

Near the blue fire.

But never like that.

Vossfang tightened softly around his forearm again.

Uneasy.

Then Kael approached quietly from behind.

Neither man spoke immediately.

Wind howled through the monastery ruins while black rain washed across broken statues and ancient stone.

Finally:

“You didn’t know it could do that.”

Rayden shook his head once.

“No.”

Kael leaned against the ruined archway beside him afterward.

“That child should’ve been worse already.”

Rayden looked toward Dawnpiercer again.

“It only slowed it.”

“Still more than most kingdoms can manage.”

That answer lingered heavily between them.

Below the monastery cliffs, black fog drifted slowly through dead forests while distant corrupted cries echoed somewhere deep within the mountains.

Kael eventually folded his arms.

“You know what people are going to start believing now.”

Rayden’s expression darkened immediately.

“I’m not a savior.”

Kael looked toward the monastery hall behind them where frightened refugees finally slept safely beside firelight.

“Doesn’t matter.”

Then after a pause:

“People don’t follow perfection, Rayden.”

“They follow the person who walks into the dark when nobody else will.”

Silence followed afterward.

Heavy.

Honest.

Rayden looked back toward the storm-covered eastern horizon beyond the mountains.

Toward the corruption.

Toward the blue fire.

Toward the thing waiting somewhere beyond the frontier.

And for the first time since Selene died...

he no longer felt like he was walking that road entirely alone.

“Get some sleep, Kael.”

Kael gave a faint tired laugh.

“That almost sounded like concern.”

Rayden stared back out into the rain.

“Don’t get used to it.”

For the first time in years...

Kael actually smiled slightly.

And beyond the monastery ruins...

the black rain finally began to weaken.

VI — Firelight Conversations

Sundered Age — Year 1040

The black rain weakened near midnight.

Not fully stopping.

But no longer hammering the monastery like the sky itself wanted them dead.

Inside the ruined halls, exhausted refugees finally slept beside scattered firelight.

Children rested beneath patched blankets while wounded civilians leaned against ancient stone pillars blackened by age and moisture.

For the first time in days...

nobody was running.

Kael remained awake anyway.

The older warrior sat near the monastery entrance repairing leather straps across the massive tower shield while his warhammer rested beside him against the wall.

Even resting, he looked prepared for another siege.

Nearby, several refugees had already started calling him “Commander” without realizing it.

Kael pretended not to notice.

Rayden sat farther beyond the ruined archway overlooking the dark valley outside.

Dawnpiercer rested across his knees.

Kingsbane stood planted beside him against the stone.

Vossfang’s chains hung motionless beneath his forearm.

He had not slept.

Probably could not.

The wind carried faint corruption cries somewhere deep within the mountains while monastery fires flickered softly behind him.

Then quiet footsteps approached.

Kael.

The older warrior lowered himself beside the ruined archway with a tired exhale.

Neither man spoke immediately.

Eventually Kael glanced sideways.

“You ever stop watching the dark?”

Rayden’s eyes remained fixed on the valley below.

“No.”

Kael nodded once.

Like he expected that answer.

Silence settled again.

Inside the monastery behind them, a small child suddenly asked one of the refugees:

“Do you think the black knight sleeps standing up?”

Several exhausted civilians laughed quietly afterward.

Even Kael almost smiled.

Rayden looked mildly offended.

“I heard that.”

The laughter grew slightly warmer after that.

For a brief moment...

the monastery almost sounded alive again.

Then Kael’s expression slowly hardened once more.

“You know this doesn’t end well, right?”

Rayden looked toward him slightly.

“The kingdoms are collapsing.”

“The frontier’s dying.”

“One man can’t carry all of this.”

Rayden’s gaze shifted back toward the storm-covered mountains.

“Maybe not.”

“Still going east.”

Kael studied him carefully afterward.

“Why?”

That question lingered longer than expected.

Finally Rayden reached beside him toward his pack.

Not dramatically.

Almost unconsciously.

Kael noticed the objects briefly when the pack opened.

A worn journal wrapped carefully beneath cloth.

And beside it...

a torn half-burned black cloak.

The fabric looked ruined beyond repair.

Burned edges.

Old blood stains.

Smoke damage permanently woven into the material itself.

Rayden closed the pack immediately afterward.

Kael said nothing.

He understood enough already.

The wind moved softly through the ruined monastery arches.

Then quietly:

“You lost someone.”

Rayden stared toward the darkness beyond the valley.

For several seconds he looked like he might not answer at all.

Then finally:

“Yeah.”

No details.

No speeches.

Only truth.

Kael leaned back against the ancient stone afterward.

“That explains the way you fight.”

Rayden frowned slightly.

“You fight like someone trying to arrive everywhere before death does.”

Silence followed.

Heavy.

Accurate.

Inside the monastery, the infected child from earlier finally stopped shivering beneath the firelight.

The corruption had slowed.

Not vanished.

Only delayed.

Rayden looked toward Dawnpiercer quietly.

“It can’t save him completely.”

Kael followed his gaze.

“But it bought him another night.”

The older warrior folded his arms afterward.

“Out here...”

“sometimes that matters more.”

The wind gradually weakened outside.

And slowly...

for the first time in days...

the storm clouds above the mountains began breaking apart.

Stars appeared overhead.

Faint.

Distant.

But still there.

Rayden stared upward silently.

Kael sat beside him beneath the ruined monastery archway while exhausted refugees slept safely behind them.

Neither warrior realized it yet.

But somewhere inside that dying frontier monastery...

the first true foundation of Starhaven had quietly begun.

VII — Selene’s Journal

Sundered Age — Year 1040

The monastery remained quiet long after midnight.

The refugees finally slept without fear for the first time in days.

No screaming.

No running.

No black rain hammering the walls.

Only exhausted breathing beside dying firelight.

Rayden still could not sleep.

He sat alone inside one of the upper ruined chambers overlooking the mountains beyond the monastery.

Dawnpiercer rested beside him against the stone wall.

Kingsbane remained nearby within arm’s reach.

Vossfang’s chains lay motionless across the floor.

And in his hands...

Selene’s journal.

The worn leather cover looked damaged from smoke and weather after years of travel.

Several pages near the edges had blackened from the Oaksmane fire.

Rayden stared at it for a long time without opening it.

Because as long as the journal remained closed...

part of her still felt unfinished.

Eventually his other hand reached into the pack beside him.

The torn half-burned Shadeveil cloak emerged slowly afterward.

Dark fabric.

Burned edges.

Old dried blood still stained across one side.

Rayden held the ruined cloth carefully.

Like touching something fragile enough to disappear.

Then finally...

he opened the journal.

The first pages contained medical notes.

Herbal sketches.

Treatment methods.

Very Selene.

Then gradually...

the writing became more personal.

“Rayden nearly broke his arm again today trying to carry two injured miners by himself.”

His expression shifted faintly.

“He pretends he dislikes people.”

“He is terrible at pretending.”

Another page.

“He still apologizes to graves when nobody is looking.”

Rayden stopped reading briefly after that.

The room suddenly felt smaller somehow.

Outside the broken monastery windows, distant winds moved softly through the mountains.

Then another entry.

“I asked him once what kind of place he would build if the wars ever ended.”

“He said somewhere children could sleep without learning what monsters sound like.”

Rayden’s grip tightened slightly against the journal pages.

“He thinks he is becoming something terrible.”

“But people like Rayden only become monsters when the world leaves nobody else willing to protect what matters.”

Silence filled the ruined chamber afterward.

Then came the final intact entry.

The handwriting looked shakier.

Hurried.

“If anything happens to me...”

Rayden froze immediately.

“...I need him to understand something.”

“He was never meant to carry the world alone.”

“And if he survives this...”

“...I hope someday he stops wandering long enough to build the home he keeps pretending he does not dream about.”

The remaining page beneath that had burned away almost completely.

Only scattered fragments remained.

Rayden stared at the ruined paper for a very long time.

Then quietly...

something inside him finally cracked.

Not rage.

Not violence.

Grief.

Real grief.

For the first time since Oaksmane...

Rayden lowered his head against the half-burned Shadeveil cloak and simply sat there in silence while the monastery winds moved softly through the dark.

No screaming.

No breaking walls.

No Kingsbane.

Only a twenty-year-old man finally allowing himself to mourn someone he could not save.

Outside the ruined chamber entrance...

Kael stood silently in the corridor.

He had arrived minutes earlier.

And quietly chose not to enter.

Instead the older warrior simply walked away again without a word.

Giving grief the privacy war rarely allowed.

Far above the monastery ruins...

the clouds finally broke completely.

And for the first time in many nights...

the stars shone clearly across the Sundered Age sky.

VIII — The Star-Eyed Huntress

Sundered Age — Year 1040

The stars remained visible until dawn.

That alone felt unnatural after weeks of black rain, frozen roads, and corruption-darkened skies.

By morning, the monastery looked less like sanctuary and more like a wound the mountains had forgotten to close.

Refugees packed quietly beneath broken arches while Kael organized the convoy with tired precision. Rayden stood near the outer steps, Dawnpiercer at his waist, shield secured behind one shoulder beside Kingsbane, Vossfang coiled around his forearm.

He had not slept.

Kael noticed, but said nothing.

They left the monastery shortly after sunrise.

The road ahead curved south through dead pine ridges and frost-buried ruins where old waystones leaned beneath moss and ash. For the first few hours, nothing attacked them.

That bothered Rayden.

Silence on the frontier no longer meant safety.

It meant something was deciding when to move.

By noon, Kael found the first marker.

A thin strip of bark carved from a black pine and pinned back into the trunk with a sliver of pale wood.

Not random.

Too deliberate.

Kael crouched beside it while Rayden stood behind him, eyes moving across the treeline.

“Trail sign?”

Kael shook his head.

“Not human.”

Rayden’s hand drifted toward Dawnpiercer.

“Corruption?”

“No.”

Kael studied the mark more carefully.

“Warning.”

Half a mile later, they understood why.

The old southern road had collapsed completely into a ravine.

Not recently.

But recently enough that the snow had not fully hidden the broken stone.

If the convoy had continued without turning, half the wagons would have fallen before anyone saw the edge beneath the fog.

Someone had redirected them before danger reached them.

Not saved.

Guided.

That was when Rayden began paying attention properly.

The signs appeared again across the next two days.

A dead crow nailed to a branch facing west.

Three pale stones stacked beside a frozen stream.

Moon-white markings painted beneath a bridge where only someone walking low would notice them.

Every sign moved the convoy away from danger.

Every warning proved correct.

Corrupted wolf tracks near one road.

Black-veined trees near another.

A ruined village ahead where the wells whispered softly even in daylight.

Kael disliked it.

Rayden disliked that he disliked it for the same reason.

Someone was studying them.

Not attacking.

Not helping out of kindness.

Studying.

On the third evening, they found the bodies.

Four corrupted scouts lay arranged beneath a frozen cedar ridge, each killed cleanly from impossible distance.

Arrows had entered through eye sockets, throat gaps, exposed spine joints.

Every shot exact.

No struggle.

No wasted arrows.

Rayden pulled one shaft from a corpse and examined it.

The arrow was pale silverwood, feathered with something soft and moon-dark. Its tip glimmered faintly, not with fire, but with cold starlight.

Dawnpiercer hummed once at his waist.

Very faint.

Rayden’s eyes narrowed.

“Whoever fired this wasn’t protecting us.”

Kael looked toward him.

“Then what?”

Rayden stared into the trees.

“Measuring.”

That night, Rayden left the camp without telling anyone.

Kael saw him go.

Of course he did.

But the older warrior only shifted closer to the sleeping refugees and planted his tower shield beside the main fire.

Trust, Rayden realized, sometimes meant not following.

The forest beyond camp felt older than the human roads.

Moonlight silvered every branch while frost clung to pine needles like glass dust. Rayden moved quietly, but not silently.

He knew enough to know the difference.

Whoever watched them would hear him anyway.

So he stopped pretending.

“You’ve followed us for three days.”

No answer.

Only wind moving between trees.

Rayden looked upward toward a broken ridge above the forest path.

“If you wanted us dead, you would’ve tried already.”

A voice answered from the moonlight.

Calm.

Female.

Older than it sounded.

“If I wanted you dead, you would not have heard me answer.”

Rayden turned slowly.

She stood on the ridge above him with a bow already drawn.

Moon Elf.

Silver-white hair fell around a face too beautiful for the ruined frontier around her, but there was nothing soft in her expression. Her eyes held pale starfire, sharp and ancient, watching him like terrain being evaluated before battle.

She appeared twenty-five at most.

But her stillness felt a century older.

The bow in her hands looked alive.

Living moonwood curved around faint glowing veins, its string shimmering like a line of captured night.

Eclipsing Whisper.

Rayden did not know the name yet.

But he understood immediately that the weapon was not ordinary.

Twin enchanted daggers rested at her hips, positioned for fast draw, not decoration.

She looked at Kingsbane first.

Then Dawnpiercer.

Then Vossfang.

No awe crossed her face.

That surprised Rayden more than fear would have.

“Most humans stand differently when carrying relics.”

Rayden said nothing.

Her gaze returned to his face.

“They either become smaller beneath them...”

“or begin believing the weapons make them larger.”

Rayden’s hand lowered from Dawnpiercer’s hilt.

“And me?”

The Moon Elf studied him for several seconds.

“You look tired enough to know the difference.”

Something moved beneath the snow behind Rayden.

He sensed it half a heartbeat before it rose.

Corrupted crawler.

Thin limbs.

Black crystal ribs.

Mouth opening too wide.

Rayden began turning.

The arrow reached first.

Moonlight split the air.

The shaft struck the creature through the skull and pinned it to the frozen ground before its claws fully emerged.

Then another arrow followed.

And another.

Three shots in less than a breath.

The crawler’s limbs spasmed violently as pale markings spread from the arrow wounds across its corrupted flesh.

Weak points glowed faintly along its joints.

Rayden understood the invitation instantly.

Dawnpiercer came free.

One controlled strike.

The creature stopped moving.

The Moon Elf lowered her bow slightly.

“You see quickly.”

Rayden looked at the dead crawler, then at the glowing marks fading from its body.

“You marked it.”

“Celestial Mark.”

She said it plainly, without pride.

“It reveals weakness. Slows regeneration. Makes a wound mean more than it should.”

Rayden wiped black blood from Dawnpiercer.

“Useful.”

“Necessary.”

Her eyes shifted toward the eastern dark.

“Against this corruption, useful things die quickly. Necessary things survive slightly longer.”

Kael arrived moments later carrying his shield.

Not rushing.

Not surprised.

Just prepared.

His eyes moved from Rayden to the Moon Elf above them.

“Friend?”

Rayden kept watching her.

“Undecided.”

The Moon Elf stepped down from the ridge with impossible grace, boots barely disturbing the frost.

“Accurate.”

Kael’s expression remained controlled, but his grip tightened on the shield.

“Name.”

She looked at him then.

Really looked.

As if reading the history of every wall he had failed to hold.

“Lyria Valen.”

Then her gaze returned to Rayden.

“Moon Elf ranger of the Elderwood.”

Kael went very still.

The Elderwood was almost myth in human territories.

A civilization that appeared on old maps only when it wanted to be remembered.

Rayden had heard soldiers laugh about it in taverns.

Hidden forests.

Immortal archers.

Moonlit borders where armies disappeared.

Most humans called those stories superstition.

Standing before Lyria, Rayden suddenly believed every one of them had been softened for comfort.

“Why are you here?”

Rayden asked.

Lyria’s expression did not change.

“To learn what kind of world is forming beyond the forest.”

Kael frowned slightly.

“And?”

Lyria looked past them toward the refugee fires hidden beyond the trees.

“A dying one.”

The answer settled coldly between them.

Not dramatic.

Not cruel.

Simply observed.

That made it worse.

Rayden stepped closer.

“You know this corruption.”

For the first time, something changed in Lyria’s face.

Small.

Almost invisible.

But there.

Memory.

“I know its first language.”

Neither man spoke.

Lyria looked toward the dead crawler again.

“When I was nineteen, the southern Elderwood began turning against natural life. Trees grew inward instead of upward. Deer birthed things with too many eyes. Rivers reflected stars that were not in the sky.”

Her voice remained steady.

That made the horror sharper.

“My reconnaissance unit entered the infected grove. Twelve of us.”

A pause.

“I delivered the report.”

Kael understood immediately.

Rayden did too.

Lyria’s eyes found Rayden again.

“Human kingdoms are calling this a frontier sickness. A border instability. A plague.”

Her voice lowered slightly.

“They are wrong.”

“This is how land begins dying before people admit the war has already started.”

Rayden felt the weight of Selene’s journal still hidden in his pack.

The burned cloak.

The child Dawnpiercer had only delayed from turning.

“Can it be cured?”

Lyria looked at him for a long moment.

Not unkindly.

But without mercy.

“Not by swords.”

The answer landed exactly where Rayden did not want it to.

Kael glanced toward the camp.

“We have infected among us.”

“Early exposure?”

“One child.”

Lyria’s expression tightened faintly.

“Then keep him warm. Keep him away from black rain. Keep him from fear when fever rises.”

Rayden stared at her.

“Fear matters?”

“Fear opens doors inside the mind.”

She looked east.

“The Void knows how to knock.”

The word hung in the cold air.

Void.

Rayden had heard it before in fragments.

Velkan’s reports.

Blackwall markings.

Corrupted whispers.

But from Lyria’s mouth, it sounded less like theory.

More like memory.

Kael exhaled slowly.

“You’ve been watching us because of the corruption.”

Lyria shook her head once.

“Partly.”

Her gaze shifted to Rayden again.

“Mostly because of him.”

Rayden’s expression hardened.

“Why?”

“Because powerful men walking through collapsing worlds usually become one of three things.”

She counted softly.

“Kings.”

“Monsters.”

“Graves.”

A faint breeze moved silver hair across her cheek.

“I wanted to know which direction you were leaning.”

Rayden almost laughed.

It came out colder than humor.

“And?”

Lyria glanced toward the distant camp again, where refugees slept around low fires because Rayden and Kael had kept them alive one more night.

“Still undecided.”

Kael gave a quiet grunt.

“Honest, at least.”

Lyria looked at him.

“You are easier.”

Kael frowned.

“Easier?”

“You are a wall.”

Her eyes returned to Rayden.

“He is a fire pretending it knows where to stop burning.”

Rayden said nothing.

Because once again, someone older than him had spoken too accurately.

A distant howl rose from the eastern forest.

Then another.

Then many.

The convoy stirred behind them.

Kael immediately turned toward camp.

Rayden’s hand went to Dawnpiercer first.

Lyria noticed.

Not Kingsbane.

The protector first.

Something in her expression shifted.

Not trust.

But adjustment.

Corrupted shapes began moving between the trees below.

A small pack.

Fast.

Trying to circle toward the sleeping refugees.

Rayden moved to intercept, but Lyria raised one hand.

“No.”

He stopped only because her voice carried certainty, not command.

Lyria lifted Eclipsing Whisper.

The bow bent silently.

Moonlight gathered along the arrowhead.

Then she fired.

One arrow became five.

The shots split mid-flight like silver birds breaking formation, each finding a different target through trees, fog, and darkness.

Five corrupted beasts collapsed before the first one finished its leap.

Kael stared despite himself.

Rayden did not.

He watched her hands instead.

Calm.

Controlled.

No wasted motion.

A hundred years of war condensed into silence.

One beast survived the volley and broke toward the campfire glow.

Lyria’s eyes narrowed.

A pale mark ignited briefly over the creature’s ribs.

Celestial Mark.

Rayden moved instantly.

Vossfang launched around a tree trunk, pulling him forward as Dawnpiercer came up in a clean rising arc.

He struck the marked point exactly.

The corrupted beast split open and crashed dead into the snow before reaching the refugees.

Lyria lowered her bow.

For the first time, she looked almost satisfied.

“You listen in battle.”

Rayden cleaned Dawnpiercer on the snow.

“When advice is useful.”

“Good.”

She turned toward the camp.

“Then listen now. Your convoy cannot continue west blindly. The road ahead is infected in layers. Surface tracks are bait. The safer route is north by the old riverbed.”

Kael studied her carefully.

“And you know this because?”

Lyria began walking past them toward the refugee fires.

“Because I mapped it while you were arguing with mercenaries and bleeding on each other.”

Kael looked toward Rayden.

Rayden looked back.

Neither had a response ready.

By dawn, Lyria had not joined them.

Not officially.

She did not swear loyalty.

Did not offer friendship.

Did not sit beside the fire pretending trust had already been earned.

She simply walked ahead of the convoy at a distance, Eclipsing Whisper in hand, silver hair moving beneath the morning wind.

When the refugees began moving, they followed the route she had marked.

Kael stayed at the center.

Rayden moved along the flank.

And Lyria Valen walked where the forest met the road, not quite with them, no longer apart.

Rayden watched her silhouette between the trees.

Kael Varric looked less like a man and more like the continuation of every dwarven defender who died holding that hall centuries earlier.

Not because they killed everything.

Because the line never collapsed.

When they finally emerged from Deepforge near dawn...

every one of them looked exhausted beyond words.

But alive.

Then they saw the smoke.

Black smoke.

Rising from the direction of the Ironmane Farm.

Rayden’s expression changed instantly.

“Ride.”

The crimson warhorses exploded forward.

Marcus Ironmane had not exaggerated their bloodlines.

The beasts moved across broken terrain like battlefield thunder itself, fearless even as distant firelight became visible against the night horizon.

By the time they reached the farm...

the battle had already begun.

Bandits.

Dozens of them.

Nearly fifty raiders swarming the outer farmstead alongside chained corrupted beasts released directly into the fields.

And standing against them...

was Lyria Valen.

The battlefield belonged to her.

Moonlit arrows curved impossibly through smoke and fire while bandits collapsed from headshots before even locating where the attacks came from.

Some arrows pierced two riders at once.

Others bent unnaturally around burning wagons and shield cover.

Bodies littered the fields.

Dresh fought desperately beside terrified farmhands near the stable lines while Helna directed civilians toward the inner barns with blood across one sleeve.

Marcus rode through the chaos atop his crimson stallion like cavalry fury itself.

The massive horse smashed directly through bandit lines while Marcus struck attackers from horseback with brutal precision using a long cavalry spear.

Then the corrupted beasts charged.

Mutated wolves.

Black-veined boars.

Half-crystalized hunting beasts driven mad by corruption.

Kael rode directly into the center of the breach.

GraveTide slammed into the earth.

Dwarven runes ignited.

Then Kael roared:

“BASTION ROAR!”

The soundwave exploded outward across the battlefield.

Every corrupted beast immediately turned toward him.

All aggression.

All hatred.

Focused entirely onto Kael Varric.

He held the line alone.

Beasts crashed against GraveTide like waves striking fortress stone while Kael’s warhammer shattered skulls and limbs with crushing counterblows.

Daven seized the moment instantly.

“SPEARS FORWARD!”

The refugee fighters obeyed.

Not perfectly.

But together.

While the monsters focused on Kael, Daven and the farmhands struck from the sides systematically, bringing down corrupted beasts one after another.

Then came flaming arrows.

Bandits atop the grain towers unleashed volleys toward fleeing civilians and the stable roofs.

Kael raised GraveTide.

MAGNETIDE.

The fire arrows bent violently through the air toward the shield instead, slamming harmlessly into its dwarven steel surface while stunned civilians watched in disbelief.

At the northern fence line...

Rayden found the bandit captain.

Nearly seven feet tall.

Massive.

Chain armor.

Execution axe covered in old blood.

Rayden launched Vossfang upward around a burning windmill support beam.

The chain pulled him violently through the smoke-filled air.

Then he dropped directly onto the captain’s shoulders.

Vossfang wrapped around the giant man’s throat instantly.

The captain roared and hurled Rayden bodily across the yard.

Rayden crashed through broken fencing hard enough to split timber apart.

And for one dangerous second...

his eyes flickered crimson.

Berserker.

The captain charged.

Too fast.

Then suddenly...

an arrow curved unnaturally through the battlefield smoke.

The shot bent around two burning wagons.

Avoided three moving fighters.

And pierced directly through the bandit captain’s eye socket.

The giant man collapsed instantly.

Lyria lowered Eclipsing Whisper from the rooftop above.

Rayden looked up toward her through the smoke.

Then smirked faintly.

Lyria returned the smallest nod.

The remaining bandits broke immediately afterward.

Most never escaped.

Lyria alone killed thirty-nine raiders before the survivors disappeared into the darkness.

But victory did not feel victorious.

The northern barns burned.

Three farmhands lay dead beside the stable walls.

Half the wheat fields had turned to ash.

Marcus Ironmane dropped slowly to his knees in the mud afterward.

Staring at the burning farm his family had built over generations.

No words came out.

Only silence.

Helna stood beside him quietly with one hand on his shoulder.

Dresh looked devastated.

But alive.

At dawn...

Marcus finally stood.

Then looked toward Rayden.

“You people keep moving?”

Rayden nodded once.

Marcus looked back toward the ruined farm.

Then toward the surviving workers.

The horses.

The wagons.

The refugees.

“Then we move too.”

And so the Ironmane family joined the convoy.

Marcus.

Helna.

Dresh.

The surviving farmhands.

Alongside them came:

Twelve Ironmane warhorses.

Supply wagons.

Livestock.

Tools.

And somehow...

five terrified chickens.

Daven stared at the chickens.

“Seriously?”

Helna looked offended immediately.

“Eggs.”

Daven nodded respectfully afterward.

“Understood.”

Behind them, the Ironmane Farm burned slowly beneath the dying night sky.

But ahead...

the convoy had become something larger than frightened survivors.

Without realizing it yet...

they were becoming the first people of Starhaven.


XII — Havenfire

Sundered Age — Year 1041

The convoy buried the dead before sunrise.

Three farmhands.

Two refugees from the Hollow Frontier.

One old stablemaster who died trying to free burning horses during the attack.

No speeches followed.

No heroic ceremony.

The frontier had buried too many people already for grief to remain dramatic.

Marcus Ironmane stood silently beside the graves while ash drifted across the ruined remains of his farm behind him. The northern fields still smoldered beneath gray morning skies while half-burned fence posts leaned crookedly through the blackened earth.

Dresh looked older now.

Only fifteen.

Already carrying the face of someone who understood that homes could disappear overnight.

Rayden recognized that expression immediately.

Which was exactly why he looked away.

By midday, the convoy moved again.

Not because anyone wanted to.

Because staying beside ruins too long eventually turned memory into weakness.

The road westward curved through low mountain valleys and abandoned military routes swallowed slowly by the frontier. Spring rain fell intermittently while wagons creaked across broken stone roads lined with old watchtowers carrying banners from kingdoms that no longer existed.

Everyone looked exhausted.

Even Kael.

The older warrior still walked patrol lines every night with GraveTide across his back, but the Deepforge battle had left bruises beneath the armor he refused mentioning.

Rayden noticed anyway.

So did Lyria.

Neither said anything.

Three days later, they found the fort.

Or rather...

what remained of it.

The structure stood atop a high ridge overlooking an old river crossing where several frontier trade roads once connected before the collapse.

Stone walls still remained partially intact.

One watchtower had fallen entirely.

The western gate hung broken from rusted hinges.

Black burn marks scarred sections of the outer courtyard where old battles had ended violently years ago.

But the foundation survived.

Freshwater flowed nearby.

The cliffs created natural defenses.

Nearby plains still carried enough living soil for crops.

The surrounding forest gave hunting access without corruption spreading too close.

Kael studied the structure for nearly ten minutes without speaking.

Then finally:

“We can hold this.”

The words settled strangely across the convoy.

Not because they sounded hopeful.

Because they sounded possible.

Rayden looked toward the ruined walls quietly.

Something inside his chest tightened unexpectedly.

Hold this.

Not survive it.

Not pass through it.

Not abandon it later.

Hold.

The convoy entered the abandoned fort slowly.

The first thing the refugees did...

was sleep.

Not nervous half-sleep beside roads.

Real sleep.

People collapsed against intact walls, stable floors, wagon beds, and broken barracks while children curled beside finally-burning hearthfires without waking every few minutes from fear.

That first night...

not a single person asked where they were traveling next.

Kael began rebuilding immediately.

The older warrior walked the walls with Brek and Daven before dawn, inspecting structural weaknesses, collapsed choke points, damaged sightlines, and broken defensive platforms.

By afternoon, work crews already existed.

Dwarves repaired stone foundations.

Humans hauled timber.

Beastkin hunters cleared nearby predator dens.

Former farmhands rebuilt fences around future grazing fields.

No kingdom ordered them.

No lord commanded it.

People simply...

started building.

Marcus Ironmane transformed the ruined lower courtyard into temporary stables within two days.

The surviving warhorses adapted quickly while Helna organized livestock care, medicine storage, and quarantine sections for sick animals before corruption could spread into the surviving food supply.

The five chickens somehow survived the entire journey.

Helna guarded them more aggressively than most soldiers protected gold.

Daven became something else quietly during those weeks.

Not stronger.

Steadier.

People listened when he spoke now.

Refugees followed his watch rotations naturally while younger fighters copied the shield drills Kael taught him during the Hollow Frontier campaigns.

One evening, Rayden watched Daven correcting formation posture beside the courtyard fires while Brek demonstrated shield anchoring against a practice barricade.

Neither man realized they already looked like commanders.

Vera noticed though.

Vera noticed everything.

The former farm worker had somehow become responsible for half the fort’s organization within days.

Food records.

Sleeping assignments.

Supply inventory.

Medical distribution.

People stopped panicking around Vera because problems simply became smaller whenever she entered a room.

Lyria spent most days beyond the walls.

The Moon Elf mapped corruption spread through the surrounding forests with terrifying precision while placing hidden warning markers and scouting routes along the old frontier roads.

At night, she often stood atop the eastern tower alone beneath the stars.

Watching.

Not guarding the fort.

Studying the world around it.

One evening, Rayden joined her silently.

The sky remained unusually clear above the frontier that night.

No black rain.

No corruption clouds.

Only stars.

Lyria rested Eclipsing Whisper beside the tower stones while looking across the growing settlement below.

Fires burned peacefully across the courtyard.

Workers repaired roofs.

Children laughed near the wells.

Someone played an old frontier lute badly beside the lower stables.

Rayden stared quietly at the scene.

“Feels temporary.”

Lyria glanced toward him.

“Everything is.”

Then after a pause:

“That doesn’t make it meaningless.”

Her voice carried no comfort.

Only truth.

Below them, Marcus kissed Helna beside the stable entrance after finishing evening repairs together.

Not passionately.

Just relief.

Nearby, another exhausted refugee couple sat together beside a fire sharing bread while their child slept safely between them.

Elsewhere, two surviving farm workers embraced near the rebuilt gate after finishing patrol duty, laughing softly for the first time in months.

No one feared sleeping tonight.

That changed people.

Rayden looked away from the courtyard.

Because suddenly...

he understood something terrifying.

These people trusted them now.

Not as mercenaries.

Not as passing warriors.

As protectors.

The realization sat heavily in his chest like armor he had never agreed to wear.

Oaksmane flashed briefly through his memory.

Burning walls.

Children screaming.

Smoke.

His jaw tightened immediately.

Lyria noticed the shift in his breathing.

But she did not force him to speak.

That restraint mattered more than comfort would have.

Days became weeks.

The ruined frontier fort slowly transformed.

Watchfires burned nightly across repaired walls.

The old forge roared again beneath dwarven craftsmen rebuilding tools instead of weapons.

Temporary crop fields appeared beyond the southern ridge.

Children stopped flinching every time distant sounds echoed through the forest.

And for the first time in years...

people unpacked belongings instead of keeping them tied for escape.

The name came accidentally.

A little girl asked one evening while watching workers relight the central courtyard brazier:

“What do we call this place?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Then Dresh looked toward the fire burning at the center of the fort.

“There’s always a fire here now.”

Brek grunted quietly.

“Haven fire.”

The name spread naturally afterward.

Havenfire.

Simple.

Human.

Earned.

That night, Kael stood atop the repaired western wall beside Rayden while guards rotated below carrying lanterns between watch posts.

The fort no longer looked abandoned.

It looked alive.

Kael rested GraveTide against the stone beside him.

“You realize this stops being temporary if we stay much longer.”

Rayden watched the fires below silently.

Marcus repairing stable gates.

Helna organizing medicine.

Daven training watch patrols.

Children sleeping safely beside warm hearths.

Home.

The word terrified him.

Because homes could burn.

He knew that better than anyone.

Kael studied him quietly.

“You’re thinking about leaving again.”

Rayden gave a tired half-smile.

“Maybe.”

Kael looked back toward Havenfire below.

“You won’t.”

Rayden frowned slightly.

“Confident.”

Kael’s voice remained calm.

“No.”

“Just paying attention.”

Below them, the fires of Havenfire burned steadily beneath the dark frontier sky.

Beyond the walls, the world still rotted.

Corruption spread.

Kingdoms collapsed.

Monsters hunted the roads.

But here...

for one fragile moment in a dying age...

people slept safely.

And somewhere deep inside Rayden Cale...

the first true foundations of Starhaven finally began taking root.

XIII — Beneath Unbroken Stars

Sundered Age — Year 1041

Havenfire survived its first month.

That alone felt miraculous.

The world beyond the walls continued collapsing exactly as before.

Trade roads disappeared beneath raiders and corruption.

Entire frontier villages vanished without warning.

Black rain still poisoned eastern rivers.

But inside Havenfire...

life refused to die quietly.

The old fortress no longer looked abandoned.

Smoke rose steadily from repaired chimneys.

The forge burned from dawn until midnight beneath dwarven craftsmen rebuilding tools, hinges, nails, farming equipment, and spearheads.

Children carried water buckets between courtyards while livestock noises filled the lower stable district Marcus Ironmane had rebuilt almost obsessively after losing his farm.

The surviving warhorses adapted fastest.

Massive crimson beasts trained daily beyond the southern fields while Dresh rode among them from sunrise until dusk, usually returning covered in mud and bruises after Marcus knocked him from the saddle repeatedly during mounted drills.

Every time Dresh hit the ground...

Rayden heard Marcus shout the exact same thing:

“Good.”

“Now learn why you fell.”

Dresh hated hearing it.

Which probably meant Marcus was teaching correctly.

Kael transformed Havenfire’s defenses with frightening speed.

The older warrior somehow saw battlefields hidden inside ordinary structures.

Collapsed walls became layered chokepoints.

Old storage halls became fallback shelters.

Destroyed towers became elevated archer nests.

Even the refugees noticed it now.

Havenfire no longer looked like people hiding from the frontier.

It looked prepared to survive it.

Brek practically lived beside the gates now.

The massive young man hauled stone blocks larger than most soldiers could lift while training younger refugees in shield anchoring beneath Kael’s brutal supervision.

One afternoon, Rayden walked past the western yard just in time to hear Kael slam Brek backward with GraveTide hard enough to knock three other trainees off balance.

Brek immediately stood again.

Bleeding.

Still holding formation.

Kael grunted quietly.

“Better.”

That single word left Brek looking more proud than wounded.

Daven changed differently.

Less visibly.

People simply trusted him now.

Watch patrols moved smoother beneath his leadership.

Younger fighters copied his discipline unconsciously.

Even frightened refugees calmed faster whenever Daven handled disputes near the food lines or gate rotations.

One cold evening, Rayden found him helping reinforce lantern posts along the inner walls while Vera organized inventory nearby with increasingly exhausted patience.

“No.”

Vera pointed aggressively toward a crate stack.

“The dried meat goes away from the grain sacks unless you WANT rats bigger than goblins.”

The worker immediately obeyed.

Daven watched quietly beside Rayden.

Then muttered:

“Terrifying woman.”

Rayden almost smiled.

“You say that like it’s a complaint.”

Daven opened his mouth.

Closed it again.

Unfortunately for him...

Brek overheard everything.

The giant defender blinked once.

“Wait.”

“You two are together?”

Silence.

Daven’s expression immediately became the exact expression of a man realizing terrible things were about to happen.

Too late.

Brek turned toward the courtyard.

“DAVEN AND VERA ARE MARRIED.”

The entire lower yard exploded.

Laughter.

Cheering.

Several younger refugees looked personally betrayed.

One of the cooks actually yelled:

“THAT WAS MY LAST HOPE IN THIS FORTRESS.”

Vera looked moments away from committing murder with a soup ladle.

Daven buried his face in both hands while Rayden leaned silently against the wall trying very hard not to laugh openly.

Even Kael looked dangerously close to smiling.

That frightened everyone more than the corruption ever had.

Later that night, music filled Havenfire for the first time.

Not professional music.

Just tired survivors sitting beside fires with battered frontier instruments while children fell asleep against their parents beneath warm blankets and rebuilt walls.

A dwarf blacksmith repaired an old flute.

A beastkin hunter roasted riverfish beside the courtyard braziers.

Someone brewed terrible alcohol strong enough to remove paint from shields.

Nobody cared.

Because people were laughing again.

Rayden walked through the settlement quietly beneath torchlight while conversations and warmth surrounded him from every direction.

He saw:

Marcus kissing Helna after evening stable duty.

A refugee soldier sleeping peacefully beside his wife for the first time in months.

Children chasing chickens through the lower courtyard while exhausted adults pretended not to encourage them.

No fear.

No screaming.

No smoke from burning homes.

Just life.

The realization hit harder than any battlefield ever had.

Because suddenly...

Rayden understood what they were truly protecting.

Not walls.

Not territory.

Moments like this.

And that terrified him.

Because every home he ever loved had burned eventually.

Oaksmane.

Selene.

Veynehold.

The ghosts still followed him everywhere.

That night, unable to sleep, Rayden climbed the eastern walls overlooking the valley beyond Havenfire.

The stars above the frontier remained perfectly clear.

No black rain.

No corruption clouds.

No burning horizon.

Only endless silver light stretching across the dark sky.

Lyria already stood there waiting.

Of course she did.

The Moon Elf leaned against the stone battlements while moonlight silvered her pale hair and reflected softly across Eclipsing Whisper resting nearby.

Neither spoke immediately.

Below them, Havenfire glowed warmly beneath the night.

Watchfires.

Patrol lanterns.

Sleeping homes.

Safe people.

Lyria finally broke the silence.

“The frontier changed.”

Rayden looked toward her.

“How?”

Lyria’s eyes remained fixed on the sleeping settlement.

“Before this place...”

“people only ran.”

Her voice softened slightly.

“Now they stop.”

Kael eventually joined them carrying GraveTide across one shoulder.

The older warrior looked across Havenfire silently for several long moments.

Then:

“This won’t survive forever with just us.”

Rayden already knew.

Too many roads.

Too many refugees.

Too much corruption spreading.

Three people could not protect an entire dying frontier.

Kael rested GraveTide beside the wall.

“We need more.”

Rayden looked down toward Havenfire below.

At the people sleeping safely because somebody finally chose to stand instead of flee.

Then quietly:

“Not soldiers.”

Lyria’s eyes shifted toward him.

Rayden’s voice remained calm.

“Protectors.”

Silence followed.

Kael understood immediately.

Not armies.

Not mercenaries.

Not kings.

People willing to hold the line for strangers.

Lyria studied Rayden carefully beneath the starlight.

“How many?”

Rayden looked beyond Havenfire.

Toward the ruined kingdoms.

Toward the corruption.

Toward the dying continent still drowning in darkness.

Then answered quietly:

“As many as it takes.”

The wind moved softly through the walls afterward.

Far below them, Havenfire slept peacefully beneath unbroken stars.

Lyria looked down toward the settlement one final time.

Then calmly sat beside the fire instead of leaving.

That was her answer.

Kael noticed.

Rayden did too.

Neither spoke about it.

After a while, Rayden finally said:

“There’s someone else.”

Kael frowned slightly.

“Who?”

Rayden’s eyes lifted toward the distant north.

Toward Vaelmark.

“The Ashen Scholar.”

Kael immediately looked unconvinced.

Rayden continued anyway.

“If he’s studying the corruption...”

“then he already knows this war is bigger than kingdoms.”

Lyria’s expression sharpened thoughtfully.

Rayden looked back toward Havenfire below.

“And if we share the same goal...”

“then eventually we should find him.”

The stars above Havenfire remained clear.

Unbroken.

For the first time since Oaksmane...

Rayden Cale looked toward tomorrow...

without expecting to lose it by morning.
