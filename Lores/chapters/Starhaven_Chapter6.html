I. The Mustering at Greenveil

Greenveil — Year 1042

The war horns of Greenveil sounded before sunrise.

The sound rolled through Greywood slowly.

Deep.

Ancient.

Heavy enough to shake frost loose from the forest branches overhead.

Fog drifted through the towering trees while thousands of fires burned across the valley below Greenveil’s outer sanctuary walls.

The frontier had come to war.

Greenveil no longer resembled the quiet boundary settlement Rayden’s group had entered weeks earlier carrying Sorin’s dying body through terrified streets.

Now the city breathed like a military heart.

Army camps stretched between the Greywood tree lines.

Temporary forge pits glowed beneath canvas shelters.

Field kitchens smoked beside supply wagons while exhausted cooks stirred massive iron pots large enough to feed entire formations at once.

The smell of wet earth mixed with:

horse sweat,

forge smoke,

cooked meat,

healing herbs,

and oil used for siege machinery.

Everywhere people moved.

Blacksmiths hammered damaged armor beside wood dwarves carving replacement spear shafts from Greywood timber.

Hunters unloaded fresh carcasses from forest wagons while Greenveil herbalists carried crates of anti-infection salves between healer tents already overflowing with wounded scouts and exhausted marchers.

Nobody wasted motion.

Because everyone understood:

once the army marched west from Greenveil…

many of them would never see these fires again.

At the center of the valley, the coalition army assembled.

Four thousand militia infantry from Havenfire stood beneath layered shield formations while torchlight reflected across mismatched armor gathered from dozens of ruined settlements.

Swords.

Spears.

Maces.

Axes.

Not professional soldiers.

Survivors.

Daven Holt walked the formation lines personally with scarred discipline.

Stopping crooked shield grips.

Correcting spear spacing.

Forcing exhausted recruits to repeat formation turns until their legs nearly collapsed beneath them.

Because Daven understood something many commanders did not.

Bad formation killed faster than enemy steel.

Nearby stood Kael’s personal shield line.

Four hundred heavy infantry carrying tower shields nearly the height of their bodies.

Massive spears rested beside blunt warhammers designed specifically for crushing armored opponents once formation lines broke.

Their armor matched more closely than the rest of Havenfire’s army.

Not elegant.

Functional.

Layered defensive plating.

Reinforced shoulders.

Interlocking shield hooks.

Kael moved silently among them adjusting shield placements himself.

This was not merely infantry.

This was doctrine.

The beginning of the wall philosophy that would later define Ironpass itself.

Farther uphill, Lyria’s formations filled the elevated ridges overlooking Greenveil.

Eight hundred mixed-race archers from Havenfire stood beside:

seventy-five Greenveil wood elf longbow hunters,

and forty-five wood dwarf crossbow specialists already calibrating range markers into nearby trees.

The difference between the groups was immediately visible.

The Havenfire archers checked arrows nervously.

The wood elves stood almost motionless beneath pale forest cloaks.

The dwarves adjusted machinery with grim mechanical patience.

Lyria walked between them all equally.

Correcting posture.

Testing bow tension personally.

Adjusting firing intervals.

One nervous young human archer released too early during formation practice.

The arrow shattered harmlessly against stone.

Lyria calmly took the bow from his trembling hands.

Then fired three arrows in rapid succession.

All three struck the same knot in a distant tree nearly two hundred meters uphill.

No speech followed afterward.

The young archer simply nodded once shakily and reset his stance.

Near Greenveil’s northern edge…

people made room automatically when the Ashen Guards passed.

Only thirty-three.

And yet they carried themselves with enough quiet certainty to unsettle entire formations simply by walking near them.

Silver purification chains.

Ash-marked armor.

Execution blades wrapped in pale cloth.

Lena Crest directed recruits preparing anti-corruption emergency kits while Seraphine moved silently between them drawing purification symbols across supply wagons, medical tents, and water barrels.

Not ceremony.

Preparation.

Several soldiers instinctively touched protective charms when she passed.

Others looked away completely.

Because everyone who survived Moonfall Lake understood something now.

Corruption did not care how brave you were.

It did not care how strong.

It only cared whether you were protected.

One by one, soldiers stepped forward before the Ashen Guards in complete silence.

No formation calls.

No conversation.

Only the sound of silver ash brushing against steel.

Ashen Guard members moved slowly through the waiting lines marking armor piece by piece with anti-corruption sigils.

Breastplates.

Helmets.

Shield interiors.

Even weapon handles.

Pale geometric symbols spread across metal beneath torchlight while purification incense burned continuously nearby.

Some soldiers closed their eyes while the markings were applied.

Others whispered prayers beneath their breath.

A few visibly trembled.

Because everyone understood what the sigils truly meant.

If corruption breached the battlefield lines…

these markings might become the only reason their bodies remained their own after death.

One young militia recruit froze completely when Lena Crest reached for his helmet.

The Ashen Guard captain paused.

“First battle?”

The young soldier nodded silently.

Lena finished carving the sigil across the front of his armor with calm precision.

“Stay near the shield wall.”

“If the corruption reaches your eyes…”

She tied a silver purification ribbon around his wrist.

“…run toward us.”

The recruit swallowed hard and nodded again.

Nearby, Seraphine continued marking armor personally without looking up once.

Her pale fingers moved across steel with mechanical familiarity born from too many battlefields already lost elsewhere.

Not blessing soldiers.

Preparing them for what happened after death if the lines broke.

If Seraphine Mire walked calmly into battle…

then the corruption ahead was already terrible beyond imagination.

Far above Greenveil, the sky thundered.

Heads lifted across the camp immediately.

Then the drakes appeared.

One hundred and twenty armored drake riders descended through the clouds beneath Ninth Sky banners while rune-light flashed across scaled wings overhead.

The ground itself trembled beneath the landing impact.

Children hiding near the healer tents stared upward in awe while nervous horses panicked against restraints.

Sorin landed first.

The drake rider removed his helmet slowly revealing the blackened scars still crossing one side of his neck beneath the jawline.

The corruption had stopped spreading.

But it had not left him untouched.

Behind him descended the battlemage corps.

Fifty Vaelmark war mages wearing dark rune-thread cloaks stepped from formation drakes with disciplined silence while arcane staffs pulsed faintly blue through the fog.

Then the sky changed above them all.

A sound like distant thunder.

Growing.

Wrong.

Pyrewing descended last.

The ancient volcanic drake dropped through the cloud ceiling like a piece of the sun deciding to visit the earth.

Crimson scales threw amber light across Greenveil's rooftops while massive wings folded with slow deliberate control.

The landing shook the ground a second time.

Every war beast in the corrals reacted instantly.

Direwolves howled.

Dire boars screamed against restraints.

Even Grond rose to his full height and watched Pyrewing with ancient animal recognition.

Velkan dismounted from the saddle harness without hurry.

Full Drakari war armor.

Black steel and silver rune channels.

Cinderwake across his back radiating faint heat through the cold morning air.

The scholar who spent eleven years studying this war from a careful distance had finally arrived at the center of it.

He looked different here than he did in Vaelmark.

Not larger.

Committed.

And among them…

one hooded battlemage stood strangely still.

Watching.

Rayden noticed him immediately.

Not because the man stood out.

Because something felt wrong.

Familiar.

The hooded battlemage watched Rayden through the crowd without movement while soldiers and drake handlers rushed around him in chaos.

Then slowly…

the figure turned away.

Rayden frowned instinctively.

Lyria noticed immediately.

“What?”

Rayden looked back toward the Vaelmark formations.

The hooded battlemage had already disappeared into the gathering mist.

“…nothing.”

But the strange feeling remained.

At the western beast corrals, Thorin’s forces prepared separately from the rest of the coalition.

Not in military lines.

Like hunters preparing for a storm.

War beast riders secured armor onto massive dire boars while Greywood veterans calmed chained direwolves already sensing violence approaching from the west.

Eighteen war beasts stood ready.

Dire bears.

Direwolves.

Armored boar chargers.

Even veteran militia soldiers avoided standing too close to them.

One armored dire bear snapped through a thick wooden restraint post with a single bite before handlers dragged it back under control.

Nearby stood another massive wildblood dwarf nearly equal to Thorin in size.

Unlike Thorin’s ancient silence, however…

this one laughed openly while lifting an entire supply crate onto a wagon himself.

Garruk Blackroot.

Thorin’s younger brother.

His beard had been braided with beast teeth instead of iron rings while old claw scars crossed both arms openly without shame.

Three Greywood beast riders argued loudly nearby over which war boar had kicked a handler unconscious earlier that morning.

Garruk listened for several seconds before bursting into laughter.

“That means the beast still respects itself.”

Even some Havenfire soldiers laughed nervously nearby.

Thorin merely shook his head once.

But for a moment…

the enormous wildblood dwarf almost looked less alone.

Later, as the last preparations wound toward evening, someone noticed Thorin standing alone at the northern edge of Greenveil's outer walls.

Not watching the army.

Facing Greywood.

The ancient forest pressed close against the settlement walls there.

Dark between the massive trunks.

Silent in the particular way corrupted places had learned to be silent.

Thorin stood with both hands resting against the top of the wall.

Head slightly lowered.

Listening.

Grond stood beside him.

After a very long time, Thorin finally straightened.

He had lived inside that forest for years alone.

Fighting corruption one corrupted nest at a time.

Watching Greywood slowly lose ground season by season.

This march was not strategy to him.

It was the only answer the forest had left.

Garruk appeared quietly beside his brother.

For once he said nothing.

He simply stood beside Thorin the way younger brothers stand beside older ones when words are not the point.

Thorin looked toward him briefly.

Then back toward the darkening forest.

Farther downslope, Vera Holt directed the siege corps through muddy assembly grounds filled with unfinished war machines.

Twenty-five siege engines stood partially assembled beneath rain-dark canvas.

Stone launchers reinforced with ironwood frames.

Heavy bolt throwers large enough to pierce fortress gates.

Reinforced breach wagons layered with wet hide and plated timber for surviving burning oil.

And beside them…

Vaelmark’s arcane artillery crews prepared weapons most Havenfire soldiers had never seen before.

Massive rune-cannons rested upon rotating steel platforms while battlemages carved stabilization sigils directly into the muddy ground beneath them.

Arcane condenser towers hummed softly blue through the fog.

Crystal-fed repeater arrays rotated slowly beside ammunition carts glowing with unstable rune shells.

One drakari engineer shouted something urgently before an arcane rotary cannon suddenly ignited with blinding blue recoil.

A deafening burst of condensed arcane fire tore across the valley and vaporized an entire dead tree line hundreds of meters away.

The shockwave alone knocked several militia recruits backward into the mud.

Nearby soldiers stared openly.

Some impressed.

Some terrified.

Because Vaelmark did not wage war like the frontier kingdoms.

It engineered destruction.

Nearly two hundred people moved through the siege camp constantly.

Carpenters.

Blacksmiths.

Hunters.

Cooks.

Labor crews.

Because wars were not won by heroes alone.

They were won by the people carrying wood through the rain while heroes slept.

As evening approached, the coalition commanders finally gathered inside Greenveil’s central command hall.

The room smelled of wet cloaks, smoke, and old parchment.

Maps covered nearly every table.

Charcoal route markings layered across terrain sketches beside supply calculations and casualty estimates already prepared before the battle even began.

Outside, the sounds of the army never stopped.

Hammer strikes.

Drakes roaring.

Soldiers shouting formation counts.

Wounded scouts arriving.

thousands of soldiers prepared to march toward the hilltop fortress.

Then suddenly—

the lantern flames shifted.

Nyx stood inside the room.

No door had opened.

No footsteps sounded.

One moment the corner was empty.

The next moment the assassin existed there.

Mud and blood stained one sleeve of his dark armor.

Something black dripped slowly from one of the twin daggers in his hand.

Nyx dropped three severed cult insignias onto the map table.

“Outer scouts.”

Silence settled immediately.

Nyx pointed toward the western approach routes.

“The fortress is ready for siege.”

His voice remained calm.

“Anti-drake ballistae on the upper ridges.”

“Secondary walls behind the main gate.”

“Kill corridors built into the eastern breach routes.”

“Hidden supply reserves beneath the southern district.”

“Cult tunnels under the lower fortress.”

Kael’s expression darkened further with every sentence.

This was not a corrupted ruin anymore.

This was a military fortress.

Nyx paused.

Then placed one more piece of information onto the table.

"Drevak has four commanders."

The room stayed silent.

"I've confirmed one."

Nyx's pale eyes moved briefly across the room.

"An orc."

"Enormous."

"Carries a hammer that looks forged from the fortress itself."

Kael looked up from the maps.

"Rank?"

"Senior."

"Commands the eastern breach defenses personally."

"His soldiers don't move without him."

Velkan's eyes moved to the eastern approach routes on the map.

"The other three?"

Rayden asked.

Nyx's expression did not change.

"Still investigating."

A pause.

"I will know more before the assault begins."

Nobody doubted it.

Nyx then moved separately around the table.

He leaned briefly toward:

Kael.

Lyria.

Rayden.

Velkan.

Thorin.

Different whispers for each.

Different information.

No one else heard any of it.

Then finally Nyx stepped backward toward the shadows again.

Rayden looked toward him.

“You’re leaving already?”

Nyx’s pale eyes reflected faintly beside the lanternlight.

“I was never really here.”

Then he vanished back into darkness.

The room remained silent afterward.

Nobody moved immediately.

The lantern flames settled back to stillness.

Kael looked at the fortress sketches for a long time without speaking.

Velkan stood with arms folded staring at the updated route markings Nyx had corrected during the briefing.

Rayden looked toward the doorway where the darkness had swallowed Nyx completely.

Anti-drake ballistae.

Kill corridors.

Secondary walls.

Cult tunnels.

Fifty thousand soldiers.

The weight of it settled across the room.

Heavy.

Cold.

Entirely real.

Outside beyond Greenveil’s walls…

thousands of soldiers prepared to march toward the Fortress.

And somewhere far beyond the forests…

Warlord Drevak waited behind black stone walls with fifty thousand soldiers beneath the shadow of the falling star.

II. The Road Through Greywood

The army left Greenveil beneath fog and iron.

War horns echoed through Greywood one final time as the coalition columns began moving west through the forest roads.

Thousands of boots crushed wet leaves into black mud.

Wagon wheels groaned.

Armor creaked.

Chains rattled against beast harnesses.

The sound of an army traveling through ancient woodland was not glorious.

It sounded heavy.

Tired.

Human.

Greywood watched them silently.

Massive trees towered overhead like old gods observing another generation march toward slaughter.

Fog drifted between trunks thick enough to swallow entire formations for moments at a time.

Above the moving army, distant drake shadows crossed through the clouds.

The Ninth Sky Corps circled intermittently overhead while Sorin coordinated aerial scouting through rotating flight formations designed to avoid enemy anti-drake ambushes.

Even then…

no rider flew low willingly anymore.

Not after the annihilation.

At the front of the marching column, Thorin walked beside Grond while Greenveil scouts moved through the forest ahead almost invisibly.

The farther west they traveled…

the quieter Greywood became.

Birdsong faded first.

Then insects.

Then eventually even the wind itself seemed wrong somehow.

Streams darkened.

Tree bark split black along the roots.

Dead animals appeared more frequently near the roads.

Most had not died naturally.

One deer carcass hung from a tree branch thirty feet above the ground with its stomach torn open and strange cult markings carved beneath the ribs.

The younger militia soldiers stared too long at it while passing.

Thorin did not.

He simply kept walking.

Jaw tightening harder with every kilometer west.

Near the supply columns, Marcus Ironmane cursed under his breath while trying to calm three panicking warhorses reacting badly to the smell of corruption drifting through the forest.

One of Garruk Blackroot’s armored dire boars immediately made the situation worse by crashing directly through a vegetable cart searching for roots.

The wagon overturned violently.

Turnips, potatoes, and onions exploded across the mud while two screaming cooks barely escaped getting flattened beneath armored tusks.

The enormous boar snorted proudly through the chaos while chewing an entire cabbage whole.

Marcus stared at the destruction in complete disbelief.

“That thing weighs more than the wagon.”

Nearby, Garruk burst into roaring laughter.

“That means he’s healthy!”

The dire boar snorted aggressively toward Marcus.

Marcus instinctively snorted back.

Silence followed for exactly one second.

Then Garruk nearly collapsed laughing.

The massive wildblood dwarf slammed one enormous hand against Marcus’ back hard enough to almost knock him sideways.

“HA!”

“See?!”

“You already understand beasts better than half my riders!”

Even several exhausted soldiers nearby started laughing quietly.

Marcus rubbed mud from his face with visible regret.

“I hate your entire forest.”

Garruk laughed even harder.

“Good.”

“That means Greywood has accepted you.”

The dire boar snorted again before stealing another sack of vegetables and disappearing between the marching lines while terrified cooks shouted behind it.

Later that evening, the coalition established camp beside a shallow Greywood river beneath towering black pines.

Thousands of campfires slowly illuminated the forest.

Forge tents glowed red through the darkness while exhausted soldiers collapsed beside shields still slick with rainwater and mud.

The Ashen Guards moved constantly through the camp.

Not only along the purification boundaries.

Through the wounded.

Silver lanterns swung softly beside them while pale healing incense drifted through the cold night air.

Lena Crest knelt beside an infected scout whose arm had already begun blackening around a corrupted claw wound.

The young soldier shook violently trying not to scream while dark veins slowly crawled upward beneath the skin.

Several nearby militia soldiers watched in terrified silence.

Lena pressed one hand firmly against the wound.

Silver light spread instantly across the scout’s arm.

The smell of burned flesh filled the camp.

The soldier screamed anyway.

Blackened corruption hissed from the wound like boiling oil while another Ashen Guard immediately began stitching the torn flesh closed afterward using silver-thread sutures soaked in purification resin.

“Hold him still.”

Two Greenveil healers obeyed instantly.

Farther through the camp, Seraphine moved between wounded soldiers one by one.

Purifying infected cuts.

Healing fevered burns.

Stabilizing corruption exposure before it spread deeper into the bloodstream.

Sometimes she healed.

Sometimes she merely stopped people from becoming monsters before dawn.

The soldiers understood the difference.

One frightened Havenfire recruit grabbed Seraphine’s wrist weakly while she sealed a corrupted spear wound across his ribs.

“…am I changing?”

Seraphine looked directly into his eyes for several silent seconds.

Then calmly:

“Not tonight.”

The young soldier nearly broke down crying from relief.

Nearby, Helna Ironmane worked beside the Ashen Guards without pause.

Bandaging ordinary wounds.

Resetting fractures.

Preparing herbs.

Checking exhausted mounts and injured war beasts alike.

At one point even Garruk’s massive dire bear arrived limping beside the medical fires after tearing part of its paw against broken stone earlier during the march.

Three militia soldiers immediately backed away in panic.

Helna did not.

She simply walked toward the gigantic predator carrying bandages and salve.

Grond watched her silently.

The enormous dire bear lowered itself slowly beside the fire.

Garruk blinked once in surprise.

“…he usually bites people.”

Helna continued wrapping the wounded paw calmly.

“Then he’s behaving tonight.”

Marcus looked deeply offended.

“Why does every beast in this forest respect my wife more than me?”

Garruk nearly started laughing again.

Later that afternoon, Marcus watched silently while Garruk approached one of the more aggressive direwolves near the outer beast lines.

The massive creature snarled immediately.

Hackles raised.

Yellow eyes fixed on the approaching dwarf.

Garruk simply crouched beside it calmly and pressed his forehead against the animal’s muzzle.

The direwolf relaxed almost instantly.

Marcus stared.

“…how?”

Garruk shrugged.

“You breathe before touching them.”

“Animals know fear faster than people.”

Marcus looked thoughtfully toward the creature afterward.

Then quietly nodded once.

Mutual respect settled naturally between them after that.

Farther along the column, Rayden moved constantly between formations instead of remaining near command positions.

Helping lift stuck wagons from deep mud.

Checking exhausted soldiers.

Carrying supply crates beside ordinary labor crews despite repeated protests from officers trying unsuccessfully to remind him he was technically commanding the campaign.

Rayden ignored all of them.

When one frightened militia recruit vomited from stress before a formation halt, Rayden simply handed the young man a water flask without embarrassment and sat beside him until the shaking stopped.

No speech.

No heroic lesson.

Just presence.

Nearby soldiers noticed anyway.

That mattered more.

Farther back along the marching columns, Daven Holt forced the militia formations through another shield rotation drill despite the knee-deep mud slowing nearly every movement.

“Closer.”

“Closer means alive.”

One exhausted recruit stumbled during a shield-lock transition and collapsed sideways into the road.

Daven immediately grabbed the young soldier by the shoulder straps and hauled him upright again before the formation even fully stopped moving.

“You collapse after the fortress falls.”

The recruit nodded shakily.

Daven shoved a spear back into his hands.

“Again.”

The shield line reset.

Moved.

Corrected.

Repeated.

Not because Daven enjoyed cruelty.

Because he had buried enough badly trained militia already.

Elsewhere along the supply route, Vera Holt stood knee-deep in black mud beside one of the heavier siege wagons while three dwarven engineers argued loudly around her.

The left wheel axle had begun splitting beneath the weight of an arcane cannon platform during a downhill crossing.

One more kilometer and the entire siege piece would have collapsed into the ravine below.

Vera spotted the stress fracture immediately.

“Stop the wheel.”

“Unload the rear counterweight.”

“Now.”

Two militia carpenters scrambled into motion instantly.

A dwarven engineer protested.

“If we unload the core stabilizer the barrel alignment—”

Vera cut him off without even looking up from the damaged axle.

“The barrel alignment matters very little if the entire machine falls into the forest.”

Silence followed.

Then the dwarves immediately obeyed.

Nearby labor crews continued dragging replacement timber through rain and mud while blacksmiths reheated broken wheel braces directly beside the road.

Because siege warfare moved at the speed of engineering.

Not glory.

Lyria marched mostly along the ridge lines beside the archer detachments.

Her eyes never stopped moving.

Trees.

Wind shifts.

Possible ambush points.

Flight lines.

At one point she suddenly raised one hand sharply.

The entire archer column froze.

Seconds later an arrow exploded from the trees toward the supply road below.

Lyria’s return shot split the cultist scout through the throat before the enemy arrow even landed.

The body fell from the branches twitching violently into the mud below.

Three more scouts attempted retreat immediately.

Greenveil wood elf archers erased them almost silently.

The entire engagement lasted less than six seconds.

Rayden looked uphill toward Lyria afterward.

She simply nodded once.

Already scanning for the next threat.

As dusk approached, the coalition established camp beside a shallow Greywood river.

The camp spread enormous beneath the trees.

Forge tents.

Medical fires.

Supply circles.

Beast corrals.

Drake landing zones.

Exhausted soldiers collapsed beside shields while cooks distributed thick stew through endless lines beneath torchlight.

Near the medical fires later that night, Helna Ironmane worked beside the Ashen Guards without pause while wounded soldiers filled nearly every available bedroll beneath the lantern tents.

Blood covered her sleeves almost to the elbows.

One scout screamed while Helna forced a dislocated shoulder back into place with brutal efficiency.

“Bite the leather.”

The scout failed to do so in time.

The scream echoed through the camp hard enough to make nearby recruits visibly flinch.

Helna immediately shoved a bottle into his chest afterward.

“Drink.”

“Then stop frightening the others.”

Three beds farther down, an Ashen Guard purified blackened corruption from a soldier’s infected arm while Helna simultaneously stitched an ordinary spear wound beside them without slowing once.

Healing.

Purification.

Surgery.

All happening beneath the same firelight.

At one point Marcus arrived carrying an injured warhorse limping badly from a torn rear tendon.

“Helna, this one can barely stand.”

She did not even look up from the soldier whose leg she was stitching closed.

“If it’s breathing it can wait.”

Marcus blinked once.

Nearby, Garruk burst into laughter again while Grond rumbled softly beside the fire.

“Told you.”

“The beasts like her more.”

Two Greenveil healers argued nearby over herb dosage while Ashen Guards moved silently through the camp boundaries spreading purification ash around the perimeter.

The white markings glowed faintly beneath the darkness.

No one crossed those boundaries carelessly.

Near the central command fire, Kael still studied maps.

Even now.

Lantern light reflected across damp parchment while charcoal markings filled nearly every remaining empty space.

Elevation calculations.

Siege ranges.

Food estimates.

Velkan stepped beside him quietly.

For a long moment neither spoke.

Then finally:

“You haven’t slept.”

Kael kept studying the map.

“Neither have you.”

Velkan almost smiled faintly.

Somewhere deeper in the camp, Dren Solm played softly beside a smaller fire surrounded by exhausted soldiers.

Not war songs.

Old frontier ballads.

Songs about roads.

Cold weather.

People surviving hard winters together.

The music drifted quietly through the massive camp beneath the trees.

For a little while…

the army almost sounded peaceful.

At the outer edge of camp, Rayden stopped walking suddenly.

Someone stood near the Vaelmark battlemage fires watching him again.

The same hooded figure.

Still.

Silent.

Rayden frowned.

That strange feeling returned immediately.

Like remembering something through smoke.

Before he could approach, however, another battlemage crossed between them carrying supplies.

When the path cleared again…

the hooded figure was gone.

Rayden remained staring into the darkness for several seconds afterward.

Then somewhere beyond the camp perimeter…

a scream echoed through Greywood.

Short.

Violent.

Human.

Every soldier nearby reached for weapons instantly.

Moments later Nyx emerged from the darkness dragging a dead cult scout by the throat.

The man’s neck had been opened almost completely across the spine.

Nyx dropped the corpse near the purification boundary.

“Three more ahead.”

Then he vanished back into the trees before anyone answered.

Night deepened slowly afterward.

Most soldiers eventually slept from exhaustion.

Some prayed.

Some sharpened weapons endlessly because stopping meant thinking too much.

And far above the camp…

the drakes circled beneath moonlight like restless predators unable to settle.

The next afternoon, the army finally reached the western Greywood elevation.

The trees began thinning.

The fog weakened.

Then the road curved around the final ridge.

And the coalition saw the hill fortress fully for the first time.

The entire army slowed.

Some stopped walking completely.

Black walls rose from the mountain like part of the earth itself.

Ancient towers pierced the clouds.

Massive siege engines lined multiple defensive levels already aimed outward toward the approaching roads.

Below the fortress…

an ocean of enemy campfires stretched across the plains.

Tens of thousands.

War banners snapped across the valley beneath smoke-dark skies while distant war horns echoed from the fortress walls.

Even from this distance they could see:

siege towers,

fortified trenches,

anti-drake ballistae,

marching formations.

Not corrupted chaos.

An army.

A prepared army.

Silence spread slowly through the coalition ranks.

Because for the first time since leaving Havenfire…

everyone truly understood what waited ahead.

Rayden stared at the walls.

Not at the army below them.

Not at the siege towers or the banners or the campfires stretching across the plains.

At the walls themselves.

He had spent eleven years reading walls.

Learning what made them hold and what made them break.

Standing beside Kael through two winters and a dozen near-collapses learning to see stone the way Kael saw it.

He saw those walls now.

And understood exactly what they meant.

His hand moved slowly to Kingsbane’s hilt at his back.

Rested there.

Not drawing it.

Just needing the weight of it beneath his hand.

Kael stared at the fortress for a very long time.

Then finally spoke quietly.

“…this is going to be worse than we thought.”

III. Before the First Stone Fell

Dawn came cold across the western plains.

Fog rolled low beneath the hill fortress while thousands of enemy campfires slowly died beneath the gray morning sky.

The coalition army stood in final formation.

No more marching.

No more preparation.

War waited directly ahead now.

Across the valley floor, Kael’s shield formations locked into position beneath rows of tower shields reinforced with chained support braces hammered directly into the dirt overnight.

Heavy spear walls stretched between defensive intervals while runners moved constantly carrying range updates and terrain corrections between command points.

Behind them, Vera Holt’s siege crews finished positioning the coalition artillery.

Massive stone launchers groaned into elevation alignment.

Heavy breach wagons rolled forward beneath layered wet-hide coverings designed to resist burning oil.

And farther uphill behind reinforced rune trenches…

Vaelmark’s arcane artillery awakened.

Rune-cannons hummed softly blue through the fog while battlemages calibrated firing crystals beside massive rotating arcane repeaters mounted upon steel frames.

Condensed energy pulsed through exposed rune veins across the weapon housings like restrained lightning.

Nearby Havenfire militia soldiers stared openly.

Most of them had never seen war machines built by Vaelmark.

They looked less like weapons.

More like controlled disasters waiting for permission.

Farther along the eastern ridges, Lyria’s archers occupied elevated firing positions overlooking the valley.

Wood elf scouts vanished between the rocks almost completely while Greenveil crossbow crews established layered kill angles against potential flanking routes.

Lyria moved silently through them all.

Adjusting range markers.

Correcting elevation calculations.

Checking wind shifts personally.

No wasted movement.

No visible fear.

Only focus.

Far above the battlefield…

Pyrewing circled.

The ancient drake moved slowly through the cold morning sky at an elevation no ballista could comfortably reach.

Crimson scales absorbed the pale dawn light while massive wings held position against the wind with almost lazy precision.

Velkan sat motionless in the saddle harness.

Not watching the coalition forces below.

Watching the fortress.

Eleven years of corrupted territory maps.

Fracture patterns.

Void convergence routes.

Structural analysis of ancient fortresses consumed by darkness.

All of it narrowing now to one hilltop.

One set of walls.

One enemy he had been studying from a careful distance for longer than most of the soldiers below had been alive.

Heavier.

Older than the warlord who had built his empire above it.

Recognition.

Warhorses.

Supply beasts.

Messenger riders.

Holding.
