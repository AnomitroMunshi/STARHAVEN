I. Havenfire Rises

Sundered Age — Year 1041

Summer reached Havenfire slowly.

Not as warmth.

As survival.

The dead fields beyond the frontier walls no longer looked completely hopeless. Thin green life pushed through damaged soil beside the southern terraces while repaired irrigation trenches carried river water toward newly planted crops under Marcus Ironmane’s relentless supervision.

Nothing grew perfectly.

But things grew.

That alone felt unnatural after the Hollow Frontier.

Havenfire no longer resembled the exhausted refugee encampment that first crawled through the broken fortress gates months earlier.

Smoke rose constantly now from active kitchens, furnaces, workshops, and stables. Repaired walls stretched across the ridge line beneath newly built watch platforms while caravans from scattered frontier survivors occasionally appeared along the lower roads seeking shelter after hearing rumors of a defended settlement that accepted anyone willing to work.

Some arrivals stayed only one night.

Others never left again.

The old fortress breathed now.

Messy.

Noisy.

Alive.

Children ran through the lower courtyards carrying wooden practice swords while exhausted adults shouted at them to stop climbing unfinished barricades. Chickens wandered almost everywhere despite Helna Ironmane threatening violence against anyone who misplaced them.

Nobody tested whether she was joking.

The forge district became the loudest part of Havenfire.

Dwarven craftsmen from the frontier refugee groups had fully restored the ancient lower furnace halls beneath the western ridge. The rhythmic roar of hammers echoed through the settlement from dawn until deep into the night as tools, armor plates, horseshoes, spearheads, and farming equipment emerged from fire-blackened workshops.

Not luxury.

Necessity.

That was how Havenfire functioned.

No wasted effort.

No ornamental wealth.

Everything built there existed because someone needed it to survive tomorrow.

Kael Varric enforced that philosophy harder than anyone.

The older warrior transformed the refugee militia into something steadily approaching a real defensive force.

Forty-five trained fighters now rotated between wall patrols, gate defense, hunting escorts, and scouting watches beneath his command structure.

Not soldiers.

Protectors.

Kael corrected anyone who used the wrong word.

The morning drills became infamous throughout Havenfire.

At sunrise, shield formations filled the central training yard beneath Kael’s brutal supervision while Brek Stonewall anchored the front ranks like an immovable fortress himself.

GraveTide had changed Kael.

Not emotionally.

Physically.

The legendary tower shield looked monstrous beside ordinary equipment, its ancient dwarven runes faintly visible beneath layered steel whenever sunlight struck the surface directly.

Most recruits stared at it with open awe.

Kael used it to hit them anyway.

“If your shield falls...”

THUD.

“People behind you die.”

THUD.

“Again.”

Brek had stopped falling entirely.

The giant defender now held formation against impacts that would have flattened ordinary fighters while Daven Holt moved through the lines correcting posture, footwork, and rotation timing before Kael needed to speak.

Daven’s authority no longer felt borrowed.

It belonged to him naturally now.

The younger recruits followed him instinctively during exercises while even veteran refugees listened when he spoke during patrol assignments.

Vera noticed it before Daven did.

Which amused her endlessly.

One afternoon, Rayden walked through the upper courtyard just in time to see Vera reorganizing supply inventories while Daven attempted unsuccessfully to help.

“No.”

Vera snatched a ledger from his hands.

“You organize weapons.”

“You do not organize food.”

Daven frowned.

“I organized the grain sacks yesterday.”

Vera stared at him blankly.

“You stacked flour beside lamp oil.”

Rayden kept walking before Daven died publicly.

Even he had learned survival instincts eventually.

The southern fields belonged entirely to Marcus Ironmane now.

The massive horse breeder rebuilt his life with frightening determination after losing the farm.

Stable yards expanded weekly beneath his direction while the surviving Ironmane warhorses produced the first healthy foals Havenfire had seen since its founding.

People gathered just to watch them stand.

It felt symbolic somehow.

Dresh Ironmane practically lived on horseback now.

The fifteen-year-old rode patrols, trained recruits, hauled supply wagons, and somehow still found enough energy to challenge older militia fighters to sparring matches he usually lost spectacularly.

Marcus approved completely.

“Better bruised now than dead later.”

Rayden spent most mornings training smaller combat groups separately from Kael’s defensive formations.

Unlike Kael’s harsh endurance drills, Rayden focused on battlefield control.

Precision.

Movement.

Protecting civilians during chaos.

Dawnpiercer flashed constantly through the training grounds as he demonstrated disarming strikes, shield throws, controlled takedowns, and defensive positioning inside crowded engagements.

Every movement looked measured.

Intentional.

Then occasionally...

he switched weapons.

Kingsbane changed the atmosphere instantly every time it appeared.

The massive black greatsword moved like battlefield catastrophe given shape. Training dummies exploded apart beneath single swings while entire shield barricades shattered under impacts strong enough to shake the yard itself.

The recruits never stopped staring.

Rayden eventually planted Kingsbane into the earth beside them one afternoon.

“Most people think bigger weapons mean bigger strength.”

He rested one hand on Dawnpiercer.

“Wrong.”

“The important part is knowing when not to use them.”

The younger fighters listened carefully after that.

Especially when Rayden demonstrated how a precise Dawnpiercer strike could disable an enemy standing inches away from civilians without harming anyone nearby.

Then came the shield drills.

The children loved those.

Rayden regretted teaching them almost immediately.

By the end of the week, half the younger boys inside Havenfire were throwing wooden plates at each other while shouting:

“WATCH THIS.”

Helna threatened to poison Rayden personally if one more child broke their nose.

Lyria Valen watched all of it with increasing quiet fascination.

The Moon Elf rarely remained inside Havenfire during daylight hours. Most mornings she vanished into the surrounding forests alongside rotating scout groups, mapping corruption spread, identifying safe hunting routes, and establishing hidden warning systems beyond the frontier roads.

But evenings...

she spent teaching archery.

The sessions quickly became legendary for entirely different reasons.

Lyria did not understand human limits properly.

At least not at first.

One exhausted recruit spent nearly twenty minutes attempting to hit a stationary target forty feet away.

Lyria watched silently.

Then casually fired three arrows without fully turning toward the range.

All three struck separate moving targets directly through the painted eye marks.

Silence followed.

The recruit lowered his bow slowly.

“I hate you.”

Lyria blinked once.

“Reasonable.”

Even Kael laughed at that.

Very briefly.

Which frightened nearby recruits more than any monster ever had.

Over time, however, Lyria changed her teaching methods.

Slowly.

Subtly.

She corrected stances more patiently.

Adjusted breathing techniques.

Showed children how to fire from defensive cover during monster attacks.

Without realizing it...

she had started investing in Havenfire too.

Rumors spread faster than crops.

By midsummer, stories about Havenfire traveled across the surviving frontier roads alongside merchants, refugees, smugglers, hunters, deserters, and wandering scouts.

Most stories exaggerated wildly.

A black-armored swordsman who killed titans.

A fortress defended by immortal warriors.

A Moon Elf ghost who could split arrows in darkness.

A giant shield blessed by dwarven gods.

Rayden hated every version equally.

But one rumor interested Lyria immediately.

The story came from an older refugee scout who arrived near dusk during heavy rain.

The woman looked half-dead from travel.

Missing two fingers.

Scar crossing one eye.

Weathered leather gear patched together repeatedly over years of frontier survival.

She refused treatment until after eating.

Kael respected that instantly.

Later that night, she sat beside the western watchfire speaking quietly with several scouts while rain hammered against Havenfire’s walls.

Lyria joined the conversation eventually.

The older scout lowered her voice immediately.

“You ever hear of the shadow routes?”

Lyria’s expression sharpened slightly.

“No.”

The woman stared into the fire.

“Messages moving through kingdoms without couriers.”

“Bandit chiefs found dead inside locked rooms.”

“Slave caravans disappearing overnight.”

“Corruption cults exposed before they spread.”

One younger scout frowned.

“Who’s doing it?”

The older woman’s voice dropped lower.

“Nobody knows.”

“Some call him the Black Silence.”

“Others say he’s a ghost.”

Lightning flashed beyond Havenfire’s walls.

The woman continued quietly:

“I only know this.”

“If he wants inside a place...”

“the guards stop looking the right direction before they even realize why.”

Lyria said nothing afterward.

But her pale eyes remained thoughtful long after the conversation ended.

Late that same night, Rayden found her standing atop the eastern walls overlooking the valley roads below Havenfire.

The settlement glowed warmly behind them beneath torchlight and forge fire.

Lyria rested Eclipsing Whisper against the stone battlements.

“Havenfire can survive now.”

Rayden folded his arms.

“Temporarily.”

Lyria nodded once.

“Which means we finally have time to think beyond tomorrow.”

The wind carried distant forge sounds upward from the lower district.

Rayden already knew where the conversation was heading.

Lyria looked north.

Toward Vaelmark.

“The corruption is evolving faster than frontier kingdoms understand.”

“And Velkan Drake already noticed us.”

Rayden remained quiet.

Lyria finally looked toward him directly.

“You should go.”

Below them, Havenfire continued breathing beneath the night.

Children slept safely.

Watchfires burned steadily.

People lived.

Rayden looked back toward the fortress behind him.

Then toward the distant northern darkness.

Kael eventually joined them carrying GraveTide across one shoulder.

The older warrior listened silently while Lyria explained her reasoning.

Then finally:

“I stay here.”

No hesitation.

Rayden frowned slightly.

“You trust me alone with Vaelmark?”

Kael’s expression remained perfectly serious.

“No.”

“I trust Vaelmark less.”

Even Lyria almost smiled.

Kael looked back toward Havenfire below.

“The walls aren’t finished.”

“Militia still needs discipline.”

“And if something attacks while we’re all gone...”

He did not finish the sentence.

He didn’t need to.

Rayden nodded once.

Lyria looked toward the forests beyond Havenfire afterward.

“I’ll investigate the shadow-route rumors while you travel.”

Rayden glanced sideways toward her.

“You think this Black Silence exists?”

Lyria’s eyes remained on the darkness beyond the walls.

“Someone is destabilizing corruption networks intelligently.”

“That interests me.”

The answer sounded calm.

But Rayden noticed something else beneath it.

Hope.

Not for survival.

For allies.

Near midnight, Rayden finally prepared for departure.

Dawnpiercer rested at his waist.

Kingsbane secured across his back.

The round shield beside it.

Vossfang wrapped silently around his arm beneath dark traveling cloth.

Below the walls, Havenfire glowed warm against the endless frontier darkness.

Lyria watched him tighten the final straps.

“Vaelmark does not welcome people.”

Rayden rested one hand against Dawnpiercer’s hilt.

“Good thing I’m not visiting.”

The gates of Havenfire slowly opened beneath the midnight stars.

And somewhere far beyond the northern rivers...

the City That Watched the End waited beneath silver fire skies.

II — The Scholar of Vaelmark

Sundered Age — Year 1041

Rayden left Havenfire before sunrise.

The gates opened quietly beneath torchlight while the settlement still slept behind the walls Kael continued rebuilding stone by stone.

Only a few people remained awake.

Daven supervised the night watch beside the western tower.

Vera organized the incoming grain inventory with exhausted irritation.

Marcus Ironmane inspected stable lanterns personally despite Helna repeatedly telling him normal people eventually required sleep.

Rayden noticed all of it while tightening Dawnpiercer’s belt straps near the gate.

That was the strange thing about Havenfire now.

Even when quiet...

it felt alive.

Kael stood waiting beside the opened gate with GraveTide resting against one shoulder.

Neither man spoke immediately.

Their friendship no longer needed constant words to exist.

Finally:

“If Vaelmark tries something stupid...”

Rayden smirked faintly.

“You’ll hear the explosion from here?”

Kael looked perfectly serious.

“Probably.”

That actually sounded believable.

Lyria arrived last.

The Moon Elf carried Eclipsing Whisper across her back while several folded scouting maps rested beneath one arm alongside notes documenting corruption spread around the northern roads.

She handed the maps to Rayden quietly.

“Avoid the western valley crossings.”

“Corruption nests appeared there three weeks ago.”

Rayden accepted the maps.

Then noticed additional markings.

Shadow symbols.

Small black ink lines hidden between route indicators.

Lyria noticed him noticing.

“Possible shadow-route activity.”

“If the stories are true...”

She paused slightly.

“someone intelligent is already fighting this war from the dark.”

Rayden folded the map carefully.

For a brief moment, silence settled between them beneath the cold frontier dawn.

Then Kael finally spoke.

“Come back alive.”

Rayden glanced sideways toward him.

“You too.”

Then he left Havenfire behind.

The northern roads felt different almost immediately.

The farther Rayden traveled northwest...

the less the world resembled the dying frontier.

Not safer.

Controlled.

Old trade roads slowly transformed into smooth blackstone pathways lined with ancient rune pylons glowing faint blue beneath the overcast skies. Burned corruption zones appeared periodically beside the roads where entire forests had been sterilized completely through arcane fire.

No corpses remained.

No scavengers.

No life.

Just clean ash.

That disturbed Rayden more than ordinary battlefield slaughter ever had.

Several abandoned villages stood untouched beside the northern rivers.

No signs of attack.

No blood.

No bodies.

Only glowing Vaelmark warning sigils burned into the stone entrances:

QUARANTINED.

Further north, strange floating crystal constructs drifted silently through the skies above the roads.

Watching.

Rayden felt their attention constantly.

Vaelmark monitored its territory like a living organism.

Three days into the journey, rain began falling heavily across the northern highland roads.

Rayden reached the remains of an old checkpoint fortress near dusk.

The structure overlooked a narrow river crossing cutting through elevated blackstone cliffs beneath the northern forests.

Something felt wrong immediately.

Too quiet.

Rayden’s hand rested against Dawnpiercer instinctively.

Then he saw the bodies.

Six dead merchants near the crossing.

Not recently killed.

Partially corrupted.

Black crystal veins spread beneath their skin while several broken supply wagons lay overturned nearby.

No valuables remained.

But the corpses had not been looted properly either.

Which meant the attackers were interrupted.

Rayden crouched beside one of the bodies carefully.

The corruption looked strange.

Organized.

Not random infection spread.

Then movement echoed above the cliffs.

Corrupted mercenaries emerged from the ruined checkpoint structures overlooking the crossing.

At least twelve.

Not ordinary bandits.

Former soldiers.

Their armor carried remnants of frontier military insignias beneath black crystal growths spreading through flesh and steel alike.

One of them smiled unnaturally.

His jaw moved slightly wrong beneath the corruption.

“Traveler.”

“Stay.”

Rayden drew Dawnpiercer instantly.

The holy blade ignited gold against the rain-dark crossing.

More movement followed.

Two corrupted beasts climbed from beneath the bridge supports below.

Large.

Wolf-like once.

Not anymore.

Crystal growths pierced through their shoulders and jaws while black corruption smoke escaped their mouths with every breath.

Rayden’s eyes narrowed slightly.

Too coordinated.

These things were positioning intelligently.

The mercenaries did not charge immediately.

They surrounded.

Studying him.

Then one corrupted soldier suddenly whispered:

“He’s here.”

Rayden frowned.

“Who?”

No answer came.

Instead...

the sky darkened.

Not from clouds.

Shadow.

The sound arrived next.

Not a roar.

Pressure.

The entire river crossing trembled violently while the corrupted beasts immediately recoiled in visible fear.

Then fire descended from the heavens.

Volcanic flame exploded across the ruined checkpoint towers above the bridge in a wave of black-orange destruction powerful enough to melt stone instantly.

Rayden looked upward sharply.

And memory struck first.

Blackwall Ridge.

Burning siege towers.

Collapsed walls.

Thousands screaming beneath winter smoke.

And above the battlefield...

a massive black shape crossing through firelit clouds while arcane bombardments turned the night sky blue-white.

Pyrewing.

Rayden had only seen the drake once before.

Years ago.

During the Battle of Blackwall Ridge.

Back then, the creature had looked distant.

Almost unreal.

A battlefield rumor made flesh high above the war.

This time...

it landed directly in front of him.

The corrupted mercenaries never even screamed.

The massive volcanic drake descended through the storm clouds like living catastrophe.

Its obsidian-black scales reflected molten cracks beneath the rain while ember smoke trailed behind colossal wings large enough to eclipse the river crossing entirely.

This was not a normal drake.

It was enormous.

Ancient by drake standards.

A battlefield legend.

Pyrewing’s molten eyes fixed briefly onto Rayden before the creature landed atop the ruined checkpoint wall with enough force to shake the bridge supports below.

The remaining corrupted beasts tried fleeing instantly.

Blue-white runes ignited across the ground beneath them.

Then gravity itself twisted sideways.

The beasts slammed violently into the cliff walls hard enough to rupture bone.

And finally...

someone stepped through the rain.

Velkan Drake wore long dark arcane robes layered beneath silver-blue runic plating glowing softly through the stormlight. Ember reflections from Pyrewing’s volcanic scales danced across sharp elven features carrying the exhausted composure of a man who had spent too many years studying terrible truths.

Most unsettling of all...

he looked completely calm.

Even standing beside battlefield ruin.

Velkan studied Rayden silently for several long seconds.

Not hostile.

Evaluating.

Then finally:

“Dawnpiercer.”

His eyes shifted briefly toward Kingsbane resting across Rayden’s back.

“And Kingsbane.”

Another pause.

“Interesting combination.”

Rayden did not lower his weapon.

“You usually drop drakes onto people during introductions?”

Pyrewing’s volcanic breath curled through the rain behind Velkan.

The scholar almost looked amused.

Almost.

“Only when they survive long enough to become interesting.”

Rayden’s eyes narrowed.

Then Velkan said quietly:

“I remember the boy from Blackwall Ridge.”

Silence.

The rain suddenly felt colder.

Rayden stared at him carefully now.

Blackwall Ridge.

Years ago.

Before Havenfire.

Before Kingsbane.

Before Selene died.

Velkan continued calmly.

“Seventeen years old.”

“Holding the western breach after the third collapse.”

His gaze sharpened slightly.

“You adapted faster than the battlefield predicted.”

Rayden felt something deeply uncomfortable settle in his chest.

This man had been watching him for years.

Velkan stepped closer slowly.

No visible threat.

Which somehow felt worse.

“Havenfire currently holds approximately two hundred and thirteen civilians.”

“Forty-five trained defenders.”

“Kael Varric remained behind to complete fortification work.”

“Lyria Valen is investigating shadow-route rumors in the southern forests.”

Rayden’s grip tightened slightly around Dawnpiercer.

Velkan noticed immediately.

“Relax.”

“If Vaelmark intended harm...”

He glanced upward toward Pyrewing.

“you would already know.”

The statement carried no arrogance.

Only fact.

That somehow made it worse.

Velkan’s eyes shifted again toward Dawnpiercer.

“You use that blade carefully.”

Then toward Kingsbane.

“And that one reluctantly.”

Rayden remained silent.

Velkan looked genuinely thoughtful now.

“Most men carrying weapons like Kingsbane eventually become addicted to certainty.”

“Overwhelming force simplifies morality.”

His gaze returned to Rayden directly.

“But you keep choosing precision instead.”

Rainwater rolled slowly down Dawnpiercer’s glowing edge.

Rayden finally answered:

“People usually survive that way.”

Velkan nodded once.

“Yes.”

“For now.”

The words settled heavily between them.

Then the scholar looked toward the distant northwest horizon where faint silver lights glowed through the storm far beyond the mountains.

“Come.”

“You should see what’s approaching before deciding how to fight it.”

Pyrewing lowered one massive volcanic wing toward the road below.

Rayden stared at the drake briefly.

Then upward toward the storm-covered northern skies beyond.

For the first time in years...

even he felt small.

Not weak.

Just aware.

The world was much larger than the frontier wars he survived.

And somewhere ahead beneath silver stormlight...

Vaelmark waited.

III — The City That Watches the End

Sundered Age — Year 1041

The storm broke near dawn.

For most of the night, Rayden traveled beneath black clouds beside Pyrewing’s enormous silhouette moving silently through the northern skies overhead while Velkan guided the route without visible effort.

The farther northwest they traveled...

the stranger the world became.

The roads improved first.

Not ordinary repairs.

Precision.

Ancient blackstone highways stretched through elevated valleys reinforced by glowing rune lines embedded directly into the earth itself. River crossings no longer relied on wood or rope but floating silver bridge platforms suspended above rushing waters through arcane mechanisms Rayden could not begin understanding.

Even the forests looked different.

Controlled.

Sections of woodland had been cut away deliberately to create clear sightlines toward distant hills where massive rune pylons rotated slowly like silent watchtowers scanning the land itself.

Nothing about Vaelmark territory felt accidental.

That unsettled Rayden more than ruined kingdoms ever had.

Near midday, the clouds finally parted.

And Vaelmark appeared.

Rayden slowed instinctively.

The city rose across an enormous northern valley surrounded by elevated ridges and ancient forests split apart by silver rivers flowing through rune-controlled canals.

Massive black-and-silver towers pierced the skies above layered arcane districts connected by floating bridgeways glowing faintly beneath daylight. Rotating observatories crowned the highest structures while enormous crystalline arrays floated above portions of the city like suspended stars.

And everywhere...

there were drakes.

Winged silhouettes crossed the skies constantly.

Patrol drakes.

Messenger drakes.

Heavily armored combat drakes carrying mounted rune artillery crews.

None approached Pyrewing.

They moved aside instead.

That told Rayden everything he needed to know.

Even here...

Pyrewing was legendary.

The volcanic drake descended toward the western landing platforms overlooking the city while enormous rune anchors illuminated automatically beneath its shadow.

The landing impact shook the entire platform.

Workers immediately began repositioning massive arcane stabilization chains around the drake with disciplined efficiency while armored battlemages monitored the skies above through floating projection screens displaying distant patrol routes.

Nobody panicked.

Nobody stared openly.

Vaelmark had seen terrifying things before.

Still...

people looked toward Velkan when he walked past.

Not fearfully.

Respectfully.

That felt somehow heavier.

Rayden followed Velkan through the upper district silently while the city unfolded around him.

Markets operated normally beneath glowing arcane lanterns.

Children crossed elevated bridges carrying books instead of water buckets.

Smiths forged specialized anti-corruption equipment beside scholars debating magical calculations openly across public plazas.

No desperation.

No survival panic.

Vaelmark looked like a civilization preparing calmly for disaster instead of pretending disaster did not exist.

Rayden realized suddenly:

Havenfire survived the world.

Vaelmark studied it.

That difference mattered.

The upper observatory district overlooked nearly the entire valley.

Massive rotating structures lined the elevated cliffs while floating rune arrays projected constantly changing maps across translucent magical screens suspended in midair.

One of those screens displayed corruption movement across half the continent.

Rayden stopped walking immediately.

The spread was enormous.

Far larger than frontier rumors ever suggested.

Blackened zones stretched through entire regions beyond the eastern territories while smaller corruption fractures pulsed intermittently near isolated kingdoms like infections spreading through veins.

And near the center-western frontier...

Havenfire appeared.

Tiny.

Velkan noticed where Rayden was looking.

“Your settlement exists directly between three developing convergence paths.”

Rayden frowned.

“Convergence?”

Velkan approached the projection calmly.

Silver-blue runes shifted beneath his fingers as multiple corruption patterns expanded across the map.

“The corruption adapts.”

“Studies resistance.”

His hand moved again.

Several destroyed kingdoms illuminated red.

“When populations collapse quickly, it spreads slowly afterward.”

Then toward surviving fortified regions.

“When organized resistance appears...”

The corruption patterns intensified visibly.

“it learns.”

Silence settled heavily across the observatory.

Rayden stared at the map carefully.

It did not resemble disease spread.

It resembled pressure.

Adjustment.

Observation.

Velkan’s voice remained calm.

“The kingdoms still believe this is an invasion.”

His eyes shifted toward Rayden.

“It is not.”

“It is contact.”

The words chilled the entire room.

Before Rayden could answer, another voice spoke from behind them.

“And this is why nobody likes talking to you before noon.”

Rayden turned.

The man approaching wore dark blue battlemage armor layered beneath a long silver military coat carrying the insignia of Vaelmark’s aerial divisions across one shoulder.

Younger than Velkan.

Sharply composed.

Practical.

Unlike Velkan’s exhausted scholar presence...

this man radiated military precision.

Several rune officers immediately straightened when he entered.

Velkan sounded entirely unimpressed.

“Sorin.”

Sorin Vael stopped beside the projection platform while studying Rayden openly.

Not hostility.

Assessment.

His eyes paused briefly on Kingsbane.

Then Dawnpiercer.

“So this is the frontier swordsman.”

Rayden folded his arms.

“Disappointed?”

Sorin considered the question honestly.

“Not yet.”

That sounded dangerously sincere.

Velkan ignored both of them entirely while continuing adjustments across the corruption displays.

Sorin watched silently for several moments before speaking again.

“You built Havenfire quickly.”

Rayden’s eyes narrowed slightly.

Sorin noticed immediately.

“Relax.”

“If Vaelmark intended expansion, we would’ve annexed half the frontier years ago.”

Rayden glanced toward the massive city beyond the observatory windows.

That also sounded believable.

Sorin crossed both arms.

“The issue is sustainability.”

“Small settlements survive until larger threats notice them.”

Rayden answered immediately.

“We noticed us first.”

For the first time...

Sorin looked genuinely interested.

Velkan finally spoke again.

“That is precisely the problem.”

His fingers moved across the projection map once more.

A new set of corruption fractures illuminated across the northern territories.

Larger.

Deeper.

Organized.

One pulse appeared frighteningly close to Havenfire’s surrounding frontier regions.

Rayden’s expression hardened immediately.

Velkan watched him carefully.

“You respond emotionally to threat vectors.”

Rayden looked toward him sharply.

“People live there.”

Velkan nodded once.

“Exactly.”

The scholar walked slowly toward the observatory windows overlooking Vaelmark below.

Silver towers glowed beneath the afternoon light.

Drake patrols crossed overhead.

Rune artillery platforms rotated silently atop distant battlements.

A civilization preparing for extinction.

Velkan spoke quietly.

“The frontier kingdoms believe walls stop this.”

His gaze shifted toward the map again.

“But the corruption avoids strength now.”

“It pressures weakness.”

“Tests adaptation.”

Then finally:

“It is learning civilization.”

Silence followed.

Far below the observatory, Vaelmark continued functioning normally.

Markets.

Schools.

Workshops.

Families.

Life continuing beneath the shadow of something ancient and intelligent.

Rayden looked down toward the city.

Then back toward the corruption map.

And for the first time since Oaksmane...

he understood something truly terrifying.

The world was not dying randomly.

Something was studying how civilizations survived.

Velkan stood beside the projection table beneath the pale observatory light.

Then quietly said:

“This is not a frontier problem anymore.”

IV — Silverfire Trial

Sundered Age — Year 1041

Rayden remained inside Vaelmark for six days.

That alone felt strange.

For years, his life had become movement.

Roads.

Battlefields.

Ruins.

Temporary fires beneath temporary skies.

But Vaelmark did not feel temporary.

It felt prepared.

Every morning, drake patrols crossed the skies above the silver towers with precise military timing while rune projections shifted constantly across elevated observatories tracking corruption movement throughout the continent.

Nothing in Vaelmark existed casually.

Every wall.

Every tower.

Every patrol route.

Built for survival.

Rayden spent most of those days watching.

And realizing how much larger the world truly was.

The Ninth Sky Corps trained above the western valleys daily.

Not a massive army.

An elite one.

Volcanic drakes soared through formation patterns while airborne battlemages launched coordinated silverfire bombardments against moving rune targets suspended across the sky itself.

Precision.

Every movement measured.

Every strike calculated.

Sorin Vael commanded the drills personally from the upper command platform overlooking the western cliffs.

“Altitude six.”

“Rotate eastern wing.”

“Silverfire release on mark.”

The sky answered instantly.

Three drake-mounted battlemages descended simultaneously before unleashing concentrated streams of silver-white flame downward.

The impact vaporized the stone targets completely.

Not exploded.

Erased.

Rayden stared silently.

Sorin eventually joined him beside the overlook.

“Most kingdoms think drakes are weapons.”

His eyes followed the formations overhead.

“They’re wrong.”

Rayden folded his arms.

“Then what are they?”

Sorin answered calmly.

“Air superiority.”

The statement sounded less like pride and more like battlefield mathematics.

Rayden respected that.

Much more than Vaelmark’s scholars pretending war could be solved from libraries alone.

Unfortunately for him...

Velkan noticed that opinion immediately.

On the seventh night, Rayden received the invitation.

Not written.

Spoken.

A young battlemage approached quietly during evening meal rotations.

“Scholar Drake requests your presence at the Silver Arena.”

Half the dining hall went silent.

Rayden looked up slowly.

“That supposed to sound threatening?”

The battlemage looked genuinely uncomfortable.

“Usually, yes.”

The Silver Arena stood beneath Vaelmark’s highest observatory towers.

Ancient.

Circular.

Covered in layered runic barriers glowing softly beneath the midnight sky.

Hundreds of spectators already lined the elevated viewing platforms above when Rayden entered.

Sky Corps officers.

Rune scholars.

Drake riders.

Sorin stood near the upper barrier platforms watching silently with both arms crossed.

And at the center of the arena...

stood Velkan Drake.

The Ashen Scholar rested one hand against a long black-and-silver staff embedded into the stone floor beside him.

Cinderwake.

The weapon radiated unstable heat through the arena air itself.

Volcanic crystal pulsed faintly near the core while silverfire runes crawled slowly across the staff like burning veins beneath obsidian glass.

Rayden immediately noticed something else.

Velkan’s right hand carried burn scars.

Old ones.

Repeated ones.

The staff was hurting him.

Velkan noticed Rayden noticing.

Neither mentioned it.

Silence stretched through the arena.

Then Velkan finally spoke.
