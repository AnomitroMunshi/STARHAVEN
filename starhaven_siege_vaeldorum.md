# THE SIEGE OF VAEL'DORUM
## *The Grand Southern Offensive — Year 1052 of the Sundered Age*
### *Starhaven Coalition vs. The Southern Iron Compact — Days 1 through 35*

---

# STRATEGIC CONTEXT

The Southern Realms have been quietly uniting for eleven years.

Not through diplomacy. Through fear.

**Arch-Warlord Maltev Vaun** — former Stoneward Kingdom general, now ruler of a military confederation spanning eight Southern Realm kingdoms — has spent a decade watching Starhaven grow. He has watched the coalition form. He has watched the Nullborn conflicts harden Starhaven's armies into something he cannot match unit for unit. So he has done what every general does when he cannot match his opponent in the open field.

He built a fortress.

**Vael'Dorum** — the Compact's nerve center and southern capital — sits at the heart of the Southern Realms, constructed over twenty-two years into what military historians on both sides agree is the most defensible fortified position on the continent. Not because the walls are thickest, though they are thick. Not because the garrison is largest, though it is. Because Maltev Vaun is inside it, with 180,000 soldiers, six months of provisions, the largest array of fixed artillery south of Ironpass, and a political calculation that says Starhaven will break on his walls before it starves him out.

His calculation: Starhaven's coalition is a coalition. Coalitions fracture under prolonged sieges. Thirty days of grinding attrition at Vael'Dorum's walls, losing three soldiers for every one of his, and Drogar Stonefang goes home. Sorvane Nullis withdraws. The Karath Sultanate's desert infantry develop supply problems a thousand miles from their home sands. The coalition bleeds itself dry against a fortress that does not move, does not tire, and does not negotiate.

What Maltev Vaun does not know — cannot know, because Nyx Ravel has been preventing him from knowing for three years — is that Starhaven has been planning this siege since Year 1049.

# PSYCHOLOGICAL WARFARE — THE SEQUENCE

Rhegar Thorne does not deploy psychological assets randomly. He sequences them.

**Day 1:** Pyrewing visible at altitude — blue flame. Investment line closes with Silver Shields and Black Bastions front. Sable Conclave stages on western bank. Aethran Colossi staged at coalition rear — VISIBLE. Garrison sees three of its worst problems on Day 1 and has 35 days to think about them.

**Day 2:** Sira moves along the investment line's western section. Vael'Dorum's twelve war elephants move to the far end of their enclosure. Do not return for six hours. Handlers cannot explain it. Cyra Dunvel does not include it in the morning briefing.

**Day 3:** Zephyros becomes visible. Weather changes. Ashstride Warband rides investment perimeter. Garrison cavalry doctrine assessment: *does not apply.*

**Day 5:** Blue Orc Berserkers audible from the parapet. Southern recruits ask what the sound is. Veterans do not answer.

**Day 7:** Garrison intelligence produces Fangwalker assessment — *UNKNOWN THREAT CATEGORY.* This document does not leave Maltev Vaun's desk.

**Day 13:** Sorin's formations drop through cloud cover. 11 anti-aerial crews silenced in four passes. Response time problem identified. No solution found.

**Day 16:** Forest Golems cross the river. Crossbow fire produces no result.

**Day 17:** Thorin in the flood plain. 8,000 soldiers make a decision that training did not prepare them for.

**Day 21:** Four separate incidents of garrison soldiers abandoning north parapet watch to observe the Fangwalker staging position. The Fangwalkers have not moved. This is the problem.

**Day 23:** Preserved Dead Legions move. Twenty-one days of stillness ends.

**Day 28:** Kael's Ironwall moves for the first time. Garrison: *"Why did they wait this long?"* Answer: because they didn't need it until now. Fangwalkers still staged. Still watching.

**Day 29:** The Wrought arrive at Ring 2's northern gate. Fangwalkers move. Ring 2's defensive coordination degrades in the 40 minutes before first contact. Not from casualties. From knowledge.

*Maltev Vaun held for thirty-five days. Given what his garrison saw on Day 1, that is not a small number. It was not sufficient. Nothing would have been.*

### DAY 1 — DAWN

The coalition arrives in three columns before sunrise.

Not dramatically. Not with banners raised and horns sounding. Three columns moving through pre-dawn darkness in coordinated silence, having marched through the night specifically to reach their investment positions before Vael'Dorum's wall watch changes shift at first light.

Rhegar's reasoning: the shift change is the moment of lowest attention. Twelve minutes where the outgoing watch is thinking about sleep and the incoming watch is thinking about the day ahead. He wants the investment line established before anyone on those walls is thinking clearly.

The three columns separate at the 4-mile marker north of Vael'Dorum and move to their designated positions without a signal — the separation point was marked on every commander's map six weeks ago and rehearsed twice in the staging area north of the Ironmere border.

*What Bob Vale does at the 4-mile marker: activates the Soul Link across all three columns simultaneously. The network comes up in 40 seconds — his fastest three-front initialization. He notes this privately and says nothing to anyone. It is not the moment for commentary.*

The wall watch's first report reaches Halvorn Caust at 0342.

*"Three columns. Moving to investment positions. Estimated full encirclement in—"* The watch officer pauses. Recalculates. *"Four hours."*

Caust reads the report. Sets it down. Picks it up again.

He walks to Ring 1's north parapet himself, in the pre-dawn darkness, and looks at what is assembling outside his walls.

The investment line is already half-formed. Moving with a speed and coordination that siege armies are not supposed to move with at night. He can see the unit banners in the torchlight — dozens of them, from a dozen kingdoms, moving in formation as if they have done this together before.

Because they have.

*Halvorn Caust, standing on Ring 1's north parapet at 0354: "Who commanded the Vel'Karath siege?" "Rhegar Thorne, sir." "And before that?" "Veynehold. Year 1039." "Before that?" "The Ashen Passage campaign." A pause. "The Eastern Crossing." Caust looks at the investment line. "He's been doing this for twenty years." "Yes sir." "Then we know what the next thirty days look like." He turns from the parapet. "Inform Arch-Warlord Vaun. Full garrison stand-to at first light."*

*0811 — garrison report: "Sir, there is something at the coalition's rear position that we are having difficulty classifying."*

The Aethran Colossi have moved into their staging position.

Twenty-seven Fangwalkers at rest. Each one 40 feet from ground to shoulder. Riva Trid's riders on their backs.

**Eastern Front:** Brokk Graniteborn walks the base of the highland ridge. He does not look at the top. He looks at the rock faces, the gully lines, the approach angles. At 1047, the tunnel entrance opens.

One man. Walking.

He has seen commanders surrender before — three campaigns, two sieges, one negotiated political settlement that was a surrender wearing different clothes. He has seen the specific range of expressions that commanders carry out of positions under white flags.

Maltev Vaun's expression is none of them.

He is carrying the white flag himself. Not an aide — himself. He walks from the tunnel entrance to the center of Ring 2's interior courtyard at an even pace. Not slow, not hurried. The pace of a man covering a distance because the distance requires covering.

Rayden watches him from the parapet.

Then he rides down.

---

### THE COURTYARD — 1047

Rhegar Thorne is standing to the left of the courtyard's center position. He has been here since 1040. He watches Rayden ride through Ring 2's northern gate and watches him not dismount.

Rayden does not dismount. He stops his horse twenty feet from Vaun and remains mounted. A choice about geometry. A mounted man and a standing man have a specific relationship in a military exchange. Not dominance. Distinction. The distinction says: you are the commander of the Compact. I am the king of Starhaven. This conversation is happening between those two facts.

Vaun appears to understand this. He looks up.

---

*"Thirty-five days,"* says Rayden.

*"Yes."*

*"Against this."*

Vaun looks at the siege — the walls, the banners, Ring 2's breach, the Fangwalkers visible at the perimeter, Zephyros overhead. He looks at all of it. Then back at Rayden.

*"Yes."*

*"Why?"*

*"Because I built it to hold. Because I told thirty kingdoms it would hold. Because my soldiers believed it would hold."*

A pause.

*"And because I was not wrong about the walls."*

*"No. The walls held."*

*"The walls held."*

*"We didn't breach the Keep."*

*"No."*

*"We went around it. Through a 125-year-old brick seal and a document with seven names on it."*

Something moves in Vaun's expression — not anger, not bitterness. The specific acknowledgment of a craftsman whose work has been assessed correctly.

*"How long has the seven names been in place?"*

*"The names since Day 29. The capacity to produce them — since Year 1050."*

*"Year 1050."*

*"We were planning this siege since Year 1049."*

A silence.

*"Your intelligence director,"* says Vaun.

*"Yes."*

*"She's extraordinary."*

*"Yes."*

*"I would have liked to have had her."*

*"Most people would."*

Vaun looks at what is left of what he built.

*"What are your terms?"*

*"The garrison lays down arms. They are not harmed. Held until the political settlement is reached."*

*"The Compact."*

*"Survives. The army does not."*

*"The confederation."*

*"Ends."*

*"The kingdoms."*

*"Keep their governance. Sign the Accord. The military structures dissolve."*

*"And me."*

Rayden holds his gaze.

*"You built something remarkable."*

He says it simply. Without performance. Looking at the walls of Vael'Dorum — still standing, not breached, the three-ring structure that took twenty-two years to build and thirty-five days to take, still architecturally intact behind its architect.

*"Build something better."*

*"What."*

*"I haven't decided yet. But a man who spent twenty-two years building the most defensible position in history has a skill set I would prefer to be using for me rather than against me."*

A very long silence.

*"You're offering me a position."*

*"I'm suggesting the arithmetic resolves better if I do."*

Vaun looks at Ring 2's breach. At the Fangwalkers. At the coalition banners on what used to be his walls. At Zephyros circling. He looks at all of it with the systematic attention of a general doing an after-action assessment.

Then:

*"I'll need to think about it."*

*"You have time."*

*"How much?"*

*"The siege is over."*

A pause.

*"The rest is paperwork."*

---

### THE GARRISON — 1140 TO 1347

They come out in formation.

This is the thing that stays with everyone who watches it — more than the breach, more than the Fangwalkers, more than the Killing Ground. Thirty thousand soldiers coming out of the last position in organized columns, weapons laid down inside, each unit in formation, each formation in sequence.

Not a rout. An organized withdrawal from a position that has been stood down. Professionally. Completely.

*A Stonemark Longbeard veteran named Brek Ironshanks, sixty-three years old, writes to his son in the Holds:*

*"They came out in formation. I want you to understand what that means. They had held for thirty-five days. They had been sealed in their last room for eleven days. And they came out in formation. No disorder. No one running. No one breaking.*

*"A lot of people are going to tell you what Vael'Dorum means. They'll be right about most of it. But when someone asks you what you saw, tell them you saw thirty thousand soldiers come out of a fortress that had just surrendered and they came out in formation.*

*"That's what soldiers do. Both sides of the wall. Same soldiers."*

The last soldier emerges at 1347.

Commander Reth Hallak. Last because Maltev Vaun asked him to be — not an order, a request — and he accepted because in twenty-two years he has always been the last one out of the position when Vaun went first. Vaun went first at 1047. Hallak is last at 1347.

He stops at the tunnel entrance. Looks back into the tunnel — dark, empty, forty feet, nothing at the end except the interior of Duren's Keep. Quiet. Intact. Standing.

He turns back to the courtyard. Walks to the coalition's designated position. Holds out his ceremonial blade with both hands. The coalition officer takes it with both hands.

Hallak straightens.

He is done.

---

### DREN SOLM — 1347

*The final entry in the Iron Chronicle of Vael'Dorum:*

---

*The last soldier came out at 1347.*

*The tunnel was empty. The Keep behind him was standing.*

*I have been in this courtyard since 0900 this morning because Elysia Nocturne told Rhegar on Day 33 that it would be Day 35 and I have learned over thirty-five days to trust Elysia Nocturne's assessments about when things are going to happen. I was here when Maltev Vaun walked out at 1047. I was here when Rayden Cale rode through the northern gate and did not dismount. I was here for the conversation — not close enough to hear all of it, but close enough to watch the faces, and watching the faces was sufficient.*

*I was here when the garrison column started at 1140 and I have been here for two hours and seven minutes watching thirty thousand soldiers come out in formation, which is — I want to say this once and then not say it again because saying it more than once makes it smaller — the most remarkable thing I have seen in thirty-five days of remarkable things.*

*The fortress was not breached.*

*That is the fact I will spend the longest time explaining to people who were not here, because it is the fact they will find least believable. Every military engineer who was not at Vael'Dorum will read the record and say: how did it fall? And every military engineer who was at Vael'Dorum will say: it didn't fall. It was made irrelevant.*

*Rhegar Thorne did not take Duren's Keep. He made Duren's Keep stop mattering.*

*The difference is everything.*

---

*I have written forty-three verses for the Iron Chronicle. Every verse names someone. Every name is accurate.*

*What I will say here — in this final entry, in this courtyard, watching Commander Reth Hallak hold out a ceremonial blade with both hands — is the thing the verses cannot contain.*

*72,000 coalition soldiers are dead who were alive on Day 1.*

*I know some of their names. The chronicle names the ones I know. The ones I don't know were there too — in the Killing Ground, in the breach at Ring 1, in the covered gallery that collapsed on Day 25 with 200 soldiers inside it that Seraphine Mire entered while the rubble was still settling. They were there and I didn't learn their names and the chronicle does not contain them and that is the thing I will carry longest from these thirty-five days.*

*I should have asked more names.*

---

*The fortress is standing.*

*The courtyard is full of soldiers who held it for thirty-five days and soldiers who took it in thirty-five days and they are, at 1347 on Day 35, standing in the same courtyard in the way that soldiers stand in the same space after the thing is over — careful with each other, professional, recognizing across the space between them the thing that only people who have been in it for the same thirty-five days can recognize in each other.*

*Rhegar Thorne is at the projection table. He is reading something. In all likelihood he is reading the next thing.*

*Rayden Cale is on Ring 2's northern parapet, looking south. He is the king. He is thinking about what comes after.*

*Seraphine Mire is at the field hospital inside Ring 1's outer ward. She is not watching the surrender. She is working. There are soldiers who need her and she is there and the surrender will be in the chronicle and the soldiers she is with right now will also be in the chronicle, and this is the correct priority order.*

*Dren Solm is in the courtyard.*

*Dren Solm is writing this.*

*The tunnel is empty.*

*The siege of Vael'Dorum is over.*

---

*It was enough.*

---

*— Dren Solm*
*Ring 2 Interior Courtyard, Vael'Dorum*
*Day 35 of the Siege · 1347*
*Year 1052 of the Sundered Age*

## SENNA ASH — CLOSING

*From the personal archive of Senna Ash, Archivist of Starhold, Year 1053:*

*Dren Solm sent me the Iron Chronicle eight months after the siege concluded. No covering letter. The manuscript arrived in a waterproofed case with my name on it and nothing else.*

*I asked him why he sent it to me rather than to the press directly.*

*He said: "Because you would tell me if something was wrong."*

*I told him one thing was wrong — a minor factual error in the Day 9 entry, the ridge seizure timeline. He corrected it in the third printing.*

*I did not tell him the other thing I noticed, which is that the chronicle contains no verse about Nyx Ravel. Forty-three verses. Every other principal named. Nyx Ravel: not there.*

*When I asked him directly: he was quiet for a long time. Then:*

*"I wrote one. Three drafts. I couldn't make any of them accurate."*

*"What was inaccurate?"*

*"Every draft named something she did. She doesn't do things. Things happen in proximity to her and then the things that needed to happen have happened and she is somewhere else by the time you look for her to confirm it. A verse requires a subject performing an action. I could not find the action. Every time I found something she did, I realized she hadn't done it — someone else had, and she had arranged for that person to be in that position with the necessary information at the necessary time, and the action was theirs, and the arrangement was hers, and a verse about the arrangement is a verse about absence, and a verse about absence is—"*

*He stopped.*

*"A gap," I said.*

*"Yes."*

*"And you decided the gap was more accurate than any verse you could write."*

*He looked at me.*

*"I decided the gap was the verse," he said.*

---

*The Iron Chronicle is in its fourth printing as of the time of this writing. The gap is in every edition.*

*I have read it eleven times. Eleven times I have reached the place where the verse about Nyx Ravel would be and found the gap and understood, each time more fully than before, that Dren Solm was right.*

*The gap is the verse.*

*The absence is everything between seven names and whatever number is actually inside the fortress.*

*The arithmetic stops resolving.*

*The fortress falls.*

---

*Senna Ash*
*Starhold Archive*
*Year 1053 of the Sundered Age*
