[M-A] MARIANNE'S CLASSROOM — "EVERY LETTER IS A DOOR"

Dark fantasy illustration. Interior, morning light. A crowded orphanage
classroom — rough wooden benches, chipped slates, children of varying ages
from 6 to 13 packed together. Sister Marianne (30s, dark hair pinned back,
plain dress, ink on her fingers) stands at a battered chalkboard covered in
letters and simple sums. She is mid-lesson — animated, pointing to a word,
one child's hand already raised. Some children lean forward in interest.
Others are distracted — one small girl draws in the margin of her slate,
one boy has fallen asleep. An eleven-year-old (Rayden, dark hair, cross-

windows in long columns, chalk dust drifting in the beams. The room is
poor but ordered. Marianne is not a saint in this image. She is someone
doing an unglamorous, enormous, necessary thing without an audience.
Mood: the small heroism of refusing to let this place be only misery.
Warm morning light, amber and grey, chalk dust, ink-stained fingers.
Painterly.

a crumbling orphanage in a grim industrial district. A frozen canal runs

Two figures beside the canal mid sword drill:

Garrick Cale — late 20s, military build, dark hair, off-duty clothes. He

the orphanage carrying their own wooden practice swords, the session winding
down. Warm candlelight glows in a window above — Marianne barely visible
watching from inside.

Mood: a quiet moment between drills. A man passing something down to a boy
who doesn't fully understand it yet. Wooden swords, frozen canal, factory
smoke. Intimate and grounded. Winter dusk light — amber fading, cold grey
settling in. Painterly.

Garrick Cale (late 20s, dark hair, military jacket hung by the door, sleeves

doorway with bread, trying. A tired woman choosing to let him in.
Mood: the mundane miracle of a quiet evening — the kind of moment you only
understand the weight of after it is gone. Intimate, warm, painterly.
No grand gestures. Just two people in a kitchen at the end of a long day.

[04] GARRICK ARRIVES
Suggested: --ar 16:9
⚠ REWORKED (violence filter)
────────────────────────────────────────
Dark fantasy illustration. A burning orphanage at midnight. The front
doorway has just exploded inward. Rain and storm pour through the broken
frame — cold against fire.

In the doorway: a soldier (Garrick Cale, late 20s, military uniform soaked
from rain, sword drawn, already moving forward). He has just arrived. He is
looking at the room. This is the moment he sees it — the burning building,
the screaming children, the smoke, the chaos. His face carries the specific
expression of a man whose worst fear has already happened and who has stepped
through it anyway.

He is not heroic in this moment. He is a person the world has broken open,
now running toward the fire instead of away from it.

Inside the burning room: silhouettes of children in smoke, flames climbing
the walls, a woman (Marianne) barely visible through the orange chaos. Rayden
half-visible in the debris.

The doorway frames Garrick — storm dark behind him, inferno bright before him.
Rain and fire meeting in the threshold.

Mood: arrival that changes nothing and changes everything. Not triumph.
The specific pain of someone who came back too late and will do everything
anyway. Wide doorway framing, low angle. Orange fire interior, black storm
exterior. Rain catching the firelight. Painterly.

[05] MARIANNE'S LAST STAND — "SAVE THEM"
Suggested: --ar 3:2
⚠ REWORKED (violence filter)
────────────────────────────────────────
Dark fantasy illustration. Inside a burning orphanage at night. Firelight
and heavy smoke fill the room. A woman (Sister Marianne, 30s, dark hair,
plain dress, apron) stands with her arms spread wide, shielding two small
children pressed against the wall behind her. She is not running. She is
not moving. She is a wall made of a person.

She is looking across the fire toward someone — a soldier (Garrick) barely
visible through smoke and flame on the other side of the room, reaching
toward her. Her expression carries one word without speaking it: "Save them."

The children behind her grip the back of her dress. Firelight moves across
her face from below — warm orange, almost golden. The smoke curls upward
around her. Burning debris in the background.

She does not look afraid. She looks decided. Completely, finally decided.

Mood: the most painful kind of courage — the person who stays. The firelight
makes her look almost holy. Almost. Painterly, orange and deep amber fire,
heavy atmospheric smoke, soft ember glow. The children are the reason the
frame exists. She is the reason they survive it.
